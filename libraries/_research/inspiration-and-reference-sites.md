# Visual reference, teardown and inspiration resources

**Evaluated:** 2026-09 · **Researcher note:** The category split in two and then consolidated. One half (Mobbin, Refero, Nicelydone, Page Flows, ScreensDesign, Built for Mars) turned into paid, searchable archives of *real shipped product screens* and every one of them shipped an MCP server in the last cycle — an agent can now query proven UI instead of guessing. The other half (Awwwards, Dribbble, FWA, Behance, Savee, Cosmos) is still an aesthetics feed and has drifted further from product work, not closer. Meanwhile four names on most people's list are gone: UI Sources, Design Vault, Scrnshts and Screenlane all 301 into competitors, and Typewolf hasn't posted since Dec 2025.

## Recommendations by need
- **Default choice:** **Mobbin** — 600,000+ screens across ~1,000 iOS/Web apps plus 200 sites, and the MCP means an agent can pull "43 paywalls from shipped products" instead of inventing one.
- **Best value / best for an agent on a budget:** **Refero** — $10/mo gets 142,000+ screens, user flows, MCP, Figma plugin *and* a published agent skill. Cheapest path from "build me a settings page" to a real precedent.
- **Best for web/B2B SaaS specifically:** **Nicelydone** — 202,200+ web-app screens with full-text search *inside* the screenshots, and it shows what's behind paywalls and trials.
- **Best for interaction and transitions:** **Page Flows** — recordings, not stills. The only way to see what happens *between* two screens.
- **Best reasoning (not just pictures):** **Built for Mars** — 90+ case studies and 1,400+ examples that argue a position instead of showing a screenshot.
- **Best engineering reference:** **The Component Gallery** — cross-references 60 components against 95 real design systems, so you name and structure things the way mature teams do.
- **Best for typography:** **Fonts In Use** for the archive, **SiteInspire** for how type behaves in layout.
- **Lightest / free and current:** **Sidebar** — five links a weekday, no account, no paywall.
- **Premium/paid worth it:** Mobbin ($10/mo) and Refero ($10/mo) both clear the bar easily. ScreensDesign at $299/yr only if you ship iOS subscription apps.
- **Actively avoid for product work:** Dribbble, Awwwards, Behance, FWA, Uiverse.

## Scorecards

### Mobbin — `essential`
- **What:** A searchable library of real product screens, flows and animations from shipped iOS, Android and web apps, plus an MCP server for AI agents.
- **Verdict:** This is the reference the whole category is measured against, and it earned it by indexing what companies actually shipped rather than what designers posted. The 2026 move that matters is the MCP: an agent asked for a paywall can retrieve dozens of real ones and synthesize, which is a categorically different input than a text prompt. The free tier is deliberately crippled — no flows, no animations, no search, three collections — so treat this as a $10/mo dependency or not at all. Its own marketing site is the cleanest thing in the category and tells you something about the people running it.
- **Use when:** you need precedent for a specific product surface — onboarding, paywall, empty state, settings, filters. · **Don't use when:** you want art direction or a visual mood; it will give you competent, conventional product UI and nothing more.
- **Scores /5:** visual 5 · interaction 4 · a11y 3 · engineering 5 · maintenance 5 · docs 4 · customization 3 · perf 4 · stability 5 · originality 4
- **Evidence:** 600,000+ screens (mobbin.com/mcp) · "over 1,000 iOS & Web apps, and 200 sites — New content weekly" (homepage) · Pro $10/member/mo billed yearly, Team $16/member/mo, Finance+ add-on $399/mo · free tier: limited apps, max 3 collections, no Flows/Animations/Search/MCP · claims 2M+ designers and a logo wall of Shopify, Nike, Pinterest, Duolingo, Wise (their claim, not independently verified)
- **Looked at:** https://mobbin.com — logged-out marketing page at 1440. Tight grotesk set at ~120px with negative tracking, pure white ground, one black pill CTA and one outlined ghost CTA, no shadow, no gradient, no card radius theatrics. A greyscale logo wall at 40% opacity. Restraint held for a full viewport, which almost nothing else in this category manages. Note the *library* UI is behind login and was not inspectable.
- **Vibecode risk:** low — the source material is 1,000 different real products, so nothing about it pushes you toward one house style.
- **Link:** https://mobbin.com

### Refero — `essential`
- **What:** A curated library of real web and iOS product screens, flows and styles, rebuilt in 2026 around an AI "research mode" with MCP, a Figma plugin and a published agent skill.
- **Verdict:** Refero has repositioned harder toward agents than anyone else and the copy says so out loud — "Real product screens, flows, and patterns to help designers, builders **and AI** make better design decisions." At $10/mo for 142,000+ screens plus MCP plus Figma plugin, it undercuts Mobbin on features per dollar, and the suggested queries on its own home page ("Empty states with create-first CTA", "Paywalls with plan selection", "Search results with filter sidebars") are exactly the granularity a coding agent needs. Smaller library than Mobbin and no Android. The free tier is ~3% of the collection, which is enough to evaluate and not enough to use.
- **Use when:** you want the cheapest agent-queryable path to real product precedent, or you work inside Figma. · **Don't use when:** you need Android, or you need the deepest possible app coverage — Mobbin still wins on volume.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 4 · a11y 3 · engineering 5 · maintenance 5 · docs 4 · customization 4 · perf 4 · stability 4 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** 142,000+ screens across 400+ web and iOS products · "New screens weekly: Updated 6 days ago" (shown on pricing page, 2026-09) · Free (~3% of library), Pro $10/mo billed $120/yr, Team $12/seat billed $140/yr, Lifetime tier, students 40% off · MCP, Figma plugin and a "Refero Skill" all included at Pro · logo wall names Atlassian, Plaid, Ramp (their claim)
- **Looked at:** https://refero.design at 1440 — app-shell layout: thin left rail (New research / Web apps / iOS apps / Styles Beta), centered prompt box with Research-mode toggles, and a two-column list of suggested pattern queries. Typography is an editorial transitional serif at ~56px against a white ground; the only decoration in the whole viewport is a purple gradient on the last word and one soft-shadowed promo card bottom-left. Reads as a research tool, not a Pinterest board — which is the right signal.
- **Vibecode risk:** low — same reason as Mobbin, plus the Styles section pushes you toward explicit token decisions rather than a default look.
- **Link:** https://refero.design

### Nicelydone — `strong`
- **What:** A searchable archive of 202,200+ screens from web applications — Linear, Notion, Stripe, Tailscale, Proton, Basecamp — organized by flow and component, with an MCP server.
- **Verdict:** This is the specialist that beats the generalists in its lane. Mobbin's centre of gravity is consumer mobile; Nicelydone is B2B web software, which is what most people reading this are actually building. Two features do real work: full-text search *inside* screenshots (find every product that used the word "seat" on a billing page) and screens captured from behind paywalls and trials, which is where the interesting UI lives and where public screenshots never reach. The one real risk is structural — it appears to be a single operator (support goes to a personal address), so treat it as a subscription that could stop.
- **Use when:** you're designing a dashboard, admin, billing, team-management or settings surface for a web app. · **Don't use when:** you need mobile-native patterns; coverage is web-app-first.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 3 · engineering 4 · maintenance 4 · docs 3 · customization 4 · perf 4 · stability 3 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** 202,200+ screens (pricing page) · Free tier = 1 seat, 12 screen previews · Solo $15/mo billed yearly · Team $30/mo for 2 seats · MCP server shipped and promoted on the homepage · footer reads "© Nicelydone 2016 – 2026"; support contact is a single named individual
- **Looked at:** https://nicelydone.club at 1440 — the hero collage is the tell: a Tailscale users table with real roles, statuses and an "Invite users" button; a Proton pricing page still carrying its Black Friday banner; a Basecamp upload form mid-state. Real chrome, real data density, real promotional cruft. Compare that to Dribbble's floating device mockups. Page itself is a clean two-column split, 72px grotesk headline, black pill CTA, one greyscale logo row — competent, unremarkable, and it doesn't matter because you're here for the library.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://nicelydone.club

### Page Flows — `strong`
- **What:** Video recordings of complete user flows — onboarding, checkout, cancellation, password reset — across iOS, Android, web and email.
- **Verdict:** Stills lie about interaction. Page Flows is the only major source that records the whole path, so you see the loading state, the transition, the error, the confirmation and the follow-up email — the parts an agent building from screenshots will invent badly. Its taxonomy is unusually good: flows are indexed by *verb* (Deactivating & Deleting Account, Reactivating & Restoring, Switching Account, Agreeing To Terms) rather than by aesthetic. Cheapest of the serious paid options. It quietly absorbed Screenlane, which now 301s here.
- **Use when:** you need to get a multi-step flow right, especially the unglamorous ones — deletion, downgrade, re-auth. · **Don't use when:** you just want one screen; the video format is slower than browsing a grid.
- **Scores /5:** visual 3 · interaction 5 · a11y 3 · engineering 4 · maintenance 4 · docs 3 · customization 3 · perf 3 · stability 4 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** pricing page title states "from $8.25/mo" · covers iOS, Android, Web and Email · flow taxonomy verified on the pricing page (Account Management, Content, Social, Onboarding & Tutorials, Misc) · screenlane.com now 301s to pageflows.com · no MCP found as of 2026-09 — unverified whether one is planned
- **Looked at:** did not screenshot the logged-in library (paywalled); judged from the public pricing/taxonomy page only. Flagging that rather than pretending otherwise.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://pageflows.com

### Built for Mars — `strong`
- **What:** Peter Ramsey's library of long-form UX case studies pulling apart the real onboarding, checkout and retention flows of named companies, plus short "UX Bites" examples.
- **Verdict:** Every other entry here shows you *what* someone shipped. This one argues about *why* it works or doesn't, with the tradeoff named. That's the input that stops an agent from copying a pattern into a context where it's wrong. It has commercialized — bootcamp, pathways, quizzes, a paywall — and the free archive is thinner than it was, but £8/mo is trivial and it now ships an MCP and a Figma plugin, so the reasoning is reachable from inside a build loop. Its positioning is unusually honest about the moment: "A collection of UX research, examples and 'ohhh that makes sense' moments for people who care about design, in a sea of AI slop."
- **Use when:** you're making a judgment call — how many onboarding steps, whether to gate before or after value, how to word a cancellation. · **Don't use when:** you need visual reference; the case studies are annotated screenshots, not a gallery.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 3 · engineering 3 · maintenance 5 · docs 4 · customization 2 · perf 4 · stability 4 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** "90+ UX Case Studies & 1,400+ UX Examples" (page title) · BFM+ Individual £8/mo billed annually; Teams £32/mo for 5 seats (£6.40/person); students/educators discounted · MCP access, Figma plugin and monthly AI credits listed in the plan comparison · registered as Built for Mars Ltd, England & Wales, Co. No. 12731987
- **Looked at:** https://builtformars.com at 1440 — heavy geometric-sans headline at ~64px, near-black on white, one black CTA, and hand-drawn marker annotations on yellow highlighter over the product shot below the fold. It commits to a voice instead of a template, which in a category full of identical dark-hero SaaS pages is itself a recommendation.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://builtformars.com

### The Component Gallery — `strong`
- **What:** A free reference that indexes 60 interface components against 95 real design systems, with 2,671 concrete examples and links to each system's docs.
- **Verdict:** The most useful thing on this list that has nothing to do with inspiration. Before you build a "Tree view" or a "Combobox", this tells you what mature teams call it, what it's expected to do, and how ten different systems structured its API. That kills two failure modes in agent-built UI at once: inventing a name nobody uses, and inventing an anatomy that doesn't match anyone's mental model. Free, no account, fast, and its own page design is the best-executed in this entire research set.
- **Use when:** naming, scoping or specifying a component before implementation. · **Don't use when:** you want visual inspiration — the examples are structural references, not eye candy.
- **Scores /5:** visual 5 · interaction 4 · a11y 5 · engineering 5 · maintenance 4 · docs 5 · customization 4 · perf 5 · stability 4 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** "60 components, 95 design systems, 2,671 examples" stated on the homepage (2026-09) · free, no signup · built on Astro (data-astro attributes in markup) · ⌘K search and a light/dark toggle
- **Looked at:** https://component.gallery at 1440 — warm off-white ground with a faint engineering graph-paper grid, a high-contrast Didone-ish display serif at ~110px for the counts, hairline 1px rules forming a genuine visible grid, and pale-blue isometric line-art glyphs for each component. No shadows used as decoration, no gradient CTA, no rounded-card default. This is a designer who knows what a grid is for. Would survive a portfolio review.
- **Vibecode risk:** low — pushes you toward standard component semantics, which is the opposite of vibecoding.
- **Link:** https://component.gallery

### Sidebar — `strong`
- **What:** Five hand-picked design links per weekday, tagged, since 2012.
- **Verdict:** Highest signal-per-minute in the category and it costs nothing. Checked on 2026-09-09, the front page carried that day's and the next day's editions — genuinely current, which is more than Typewolf, Httpster or Collect UI can say. The link mix is substantive rather than decorative: a design-systems semantic-layer essay, an "Anatomy of an AI input" breakdown, a craft note on limiting detail, an MIT-licensed React component library. This is where you find the *other* things in this corpus before anyone else does.
- **Use when:** you want a standing feed that surfaces new tools, essays and libraries worth evaluating. · **Don't use when:** you need to solve a specific screen right now — it's serendipity, not search.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 4 · engineering 3 · maintenance 5 · docs 3 · customization 2 · perf 5 · stability 5 · originality 4
- **Evidence:** front page on 2026-09-09 showed dated editions for Thursday 09/10 and Wednesday 09/09 · five links per weekday, each tagged (AI, UX, Design Systems, Craft, Tools, Resources) · free, with a sponsor slot and a daily newsletter
- **Looked at:** rendered the live page rather than screenshotting; content verified as dated to the day of research.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://sidebar.io

### ScreensDesign — `strong` (situational: iOS subscription apps)
- **What:** An iOS-only library of 2,640+ top-grossing apps — full screen-recording videos, onboarding flows, paywalls, App Store screenshots — cross-referenced with revenue estimates.
- **Verdict:** The one genuinely novel thing in the category: it ties design patterns to money. You can filter to apps in a revenue band and see what their paywall actually looks like, which converts "what's a good paywall" from a taste argument into an evidence question. It has also become the consolidator of the segment — uisources.com, designvault.io and scrnshts.club all now 301 here, meaning three sites people still recommend are one product. Expensive relative to peers, and useless if you aren't shipping iOS.
- **Use when:** you're designing an iOS subscription app's onboarding, paywall or store listing. · **Don't use when:** web, Android, or anything not monetized by subscription.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 4 · a11y 3 · engineering 4 · maintenance 5 · docs 3 · customization 3 · perf 4 · stability 4 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** "2,634 apps in the top charts" (homepage) / "2,640+ top-grossing iOS apps" (onboarding modal) · Pro $24.92/mo billed annually at $299 · plan includes "Ask the Library, 200 answers a month", 2,400 create credits/yr, and "ScreensDesign MCP access (research allowance)" · uisources.com, designvault.io → 301 https://screensdesign.com/; scrnshts.club → 301 /store-screenshots/
- **Looked at:** https://screensdesign.com at 1440 — first paint is a blocking 4-step onboarding carousel over a blurred grid, which is a mild sin. The card itself is well made: heavy geometric sans at ~52px, a genuine grid-paper texture, a floating search chip pre-filled with "onboarding flows that convert" over a wall of real app icons. Reads as a research product, not a gallery.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://screensdesign.com

### Fonts In Use — `strong`
- **What:** An independent, free archive of typography in the wild with every typeface identified and the work tagged by industry and format.
- **Verdict:** The most rigorous free resource in this whole set and the one least likely to disappear. Where Typewolf gave you a daily hit and stopped, Fonts In Use built a properly catalogued archive with tens of thousands of entries and no engagement metrics anywhere — you cannot sort it by likes, which is precisely why the quality holds. For an agent choosing type, the useful move is browsing by *format* (Web, Advertising, Album Art, Editorial) to see how a face behaves at a given job rather than in a specimen.
- **Use when:** picking or justifying a typeface, or identifying one you saw. · **Don't use when:** you need layout or interaction reference — this is type only, and heavily print-weighted.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 4 · engineering 3 · maintenance 5 · docs 4 · customization 3 · perf 4 · stability 5 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** self-described "An independent archive of typography" · live topic counts read from the homepage 2026-09: Music 6,765 · Art 5,063 · Event 3,444 · Food/Beverage 3,276 · Literature 3,228 · Graphic Design 3,170 · Technology 1,587 · format counts include Album Art 4,521 and Advertising 2,253 · free, with advanced search

- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://fontsinuse.com

### SiteInspire — `situational`

- **Verdict:** Impeccable taste applied to the wrong subject for most product work. - **Use when:** you need typographic and layout reference for an editorial, studio or brand-led marketing site. · **Don't use when:** you need product UI, states, or anything with a form in it. Also note it returned HTTP 429 to a plain scripted request — rate-limited against automation.

### SaaS Landing Page — `situational`
- **What:** 960 curated SaaS landing pages, with a sibling site for in-app screens.
- **Verdict:** One narrow job, done adequately. If you're writing a marketing page for a developer tool or B2B product, this is a faster way to see current conventions than any general gallery, because every single entry is the same *kind* of page. The trap is obvious and worth stating: it will teach you the 2026 SaaS landing-page template — logo bar, three feature cards, testimonial row, pricing table — and if you follow it you produce something indistinguishable from the other 959. Use it to check convention, not to set direction.
- **Use when:** sanity-checking the section order and content of a SaaS marketing page. · **Don't use when:** you want the page to be memorable, or you need any in-product surface.
- **Scores /5:** visual 3 · interaction 3 · a11y 3 · engineering 3 · maintenance 4 · docs 2 · customization 2 · perf 3 · stability 3 · originality 2

- **Link:** https://saaslandingpage.com

### Savee & Cosmos — `situational` (art direction only)
- **What:** Two visual-bookmarking platforms. Savee indexes 6M+ curated design references and ships an MCP server; Cosmos is a searchable personal-collection tool with reverse-image, colour and visual-similarity search.
- **Verdict:** Both are excellent at what they do and neither should be used as product reference — there is essentially no interface work in either, and no states, no flows, no edge cases. Where they earn a place is art direction: if you need to establish a palette, a photographic treatment or a typographic mood before you build, these beat Pinterest. Savee's MCP is a real capability — an agent can be pointed at a board and build to that reference instead of defaulting — but note that search is metered and MCP requires a paid plan. Cosmos deserves specific credit for shipping an AI-content detector with Show/Blur/Hide controls; in 2026 that is a meaningful signal-to-noise feature and nobody else in this list has it.
- **Use when:** setting visual direction — colour, imagery, mood — before any UI work starts. · **Don't use when:** you are designing a screen. Neither will show you a real one.
- **Scores /5:** visual 5 · interaction 4 · a11y 3 · engineering 4 · maintenance 5 · docs 3 · customization 4 · perf 4 · stability 4 · originality 4
- **Evidence (Savee):** "search 6M+ curated design references"; MCP "included with every paid plan"; install is `claude mcp add --transport http --scope user savee https://mcp.savee.com/mcp`; read tools are get_current_user, list_saves, get_save, list_feed, list_boards, list_board_saves, search, view_saves, with four optional write tools gated behind explicit write consent; claims 1.4M creatives (their claim) · **Evidence (Cosmos):** search by colour and by visual similarity; explicit "AI content — Content detected as likely generated by AI: Show / Blur / Hide" control; pricing page returned "Sorry, you can't access this" to automated access — pricing unverified
- **Looked at:** https://savee.it — near-black ground, white grotesk headline at ~90px, two pill CTAs, floating pill nav. https://www.cosmos.so — off-white ground with image cards scattered in a loose ellipse around a centred ~90px grotesk headline; search field carries reverse-image and colour-picker affordances. Visible content across both: fashion photography, textile macros, gradients, product renders. Not one interface.
- **Vibecode risk:** medium — pointing an agent at a moodboard produces atmosphere, and atmosphere applied to a settings page is exactly the failure mode this corpus exists to prevent.
- **Link:** https://savee.it · https://www.cosmos.so

### Land-book — `situational`
- **What:** A large, well-organized gallery of landing pages and website designs.
- **Verdict:** Genuinely good curation and a reasonable stop for landing-page reference, but it carries a practical disqualifier for this corpus: it sits behind a Cloudflare interactive challenge that a headless browser cannot pass. An AI agent cannot read it. That does not make it a bad site — it makes it a human-only tool, which is worth stating plainly in a corpus about what agents should reach for.
- **Use when:** a human is browsing for landing-page direction. · **Don't use when:** you need programmatic or agent access — it will fail.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 3 · engineering 3 · maintenance 4 · docs 2 · customization 3 · perf 3 · stability 4 · originality 3
- **Evidence:** returns HTTP 403 to scripted requests; a real headless Chromium load renders only "Performing security verification — Verify you are human" (Cloudflare, Ray ID captured 2026-09-09) · library size and pricing unverified for that reason
- **Looked at:** https://land-book.com at 1440 — the Cloudflare interstitial, and nothing else. Reporting what I actually saw rather than what I expected to see.
- **Vibecode risk:** medium (unverified — could not inspect the corpus).
- **Link:** https://land-book.com

### One Page Love — `situational`
- **What:** A 15-year-old archive of single-page website designs, plus paid templates.
- **Verdict:** Still alive, still updated, still overwhelmingly about portfolios. Its own category counts are the honest summary: Portfolio 3,772 and Landing Page 1,998 against SaaS 150 and Startup 153. If you're building a personal site or a one-page launch page it's a deep and useful archive; if you're building a product it is 96% noise. The Style filters (Illustrative 1,654, Scroll Effects 1,321, Minimal 988, Typographic 748) are more useful than the category filters for a builder.
- **Use when:** one-page sites, launch pages, personal sites. · **Don't use when:** anything with an authenticated surface.
- **Scores /5:** visual 3 · interaction 3 · a11y 3 · engineering 3 · maintenance 4 · docs 3 · customization 2 · perf 3 · stability 4 · originality 2
- **Evidence:** live category counts 2026-09: Portfolio 3,772 · Landing Page 1,998 · Digital Product 1,520 · Launching Soon 934 · App 889 · SaaS 150 · Startup 153 · free to browse; sells templates
- **Looked at:** rendered the live index and read the category taxonomy directly; did not screenshot the grid.

### Brand New — `reference-only`
- **What:** UnderConsideration's long-running critical review of corporate and brand identity work.
- **Verdict:** The best sustained critical writing about visual identity on the internet, and almost entirely irrelevant to building an interface. Read it to learn how to *argue* about design decisions — the reviews model a discipline of judgment that most design writing lacks — not to find UI. Most content is behind a paid membership. Filed reference-only because it teaches a skill rather than supplying a resource.
- **Use when:** you're developing critical vocabulary, or working on brand identity rather than product. · **Don't use when:** you need interface reference of any kind.
- **Scores /5:** visual 3 · interaction 2 · a11y 3 · engineering 2 · maintenance 5 · docs 3 · customization 1 · perf 3 · stability 5 · originality 5
- **Evidence:** live and actively publishing 2026-09 · taxonomy covers ~45 industries; top tags include sans serif (2,409), blue (1,529), icon (1,185), uppercase (1,002), packaging (940) · subscription-gated ("SUBSCRIBE" is the primary nav CTA) · a division of UnderConsideration
- **Looked at:** rendered the live index; verified tag counts and paywall directly.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/

### Typewolf — `reference-only`
- **What:** Jeremiah Shoaf's typography showcase — Site of the Day, font lists, lookbooks and a widely-cited typography checklist.
- **Verdict:** This is the reputation-no-longer-matches-reality entry. Typewolf is still the first name most designers give for web typography, and it has not published a Site of the Day since **December 14, 2025** — nine months dead at time of research, with a visible three-month gap before that (Dec 8 → Sep 28, 2025). The back catalogue and the font lists remain excellent and are still worth reading; the site is no longer a current signal about what's happening in type. Treat it as an archive, and get your current typographic signal from Fonts In Use and SiteInspire instead.
- **Use when:** studying the archive, the font lists, or the typography checklist. · **Don't use when:** you want to know what's current — it will show you late 2025.
- **Scores /5:** visual 4 · interaction 3 · a11y 4 · engineering 3 · maintenance 1 · docs 4 · customization 2 · perf 4 · stability 2 · originality 4
- **Evidence:** Site of the Day archive on 2026-09-09 lists, newest first: Dec 14, Dec 13, Dec 12, Dec 11, Dec 10, Dec 9, Dec 8 (all 2025), then Sep 28, Sep 27, Sep 26 (2025) · site is up and serving; the homepage now carries a gambling ad in the feed slot
- **Looked at:** rendered the live homepage and the /site-of-the-day archive; dates above read directly off the page.
- **Vibecode risk:** low.
- **Link:** https://www.typewolf.com

## Rejected / avoid

- **Awwwards** — The single most misleading reference for product work, and popular enough that agents reach for it by default. Verified live: 10,336 "winning websites", and the top of the current grid is a WebGL magenta-gradient hero, a 3D stealth-aircraft render over rock, a full-bleed ocean photograph with display-serif overlay, and a truck flying out of a shipping container. Not one card in the visible grid contained a form, a table, a list, an empty state or a settings screen. These are award submissions optimized for a jury looking at a 20-second scroll — award-bait motion, unbuildable scroll-jacking, no real states, no data density, no accessibility budget. Its genuinely useful part is the Font and Colour filters for palette and type research. Everything else will make your product worse. Also runs two persistent promo layers over the grid.
- **The FWA** — Awwwards' older sibling with the same failure mode and an even heavier bias toward experiential and campaign work. Nothing here has a second session.
- **Dribbble** — Verified live: the current Popular feed is dominated by logo-presentation boards (MolGen, FIELD AI, Ascentra/Nomadic/CoPack), a pencil sketch, and two UI mockups floating inside device frames with invented data — plus a "20% off design services" promo bar and a paid Framer ad slot inside the grid. It is a hiring marketplace whose ranking signal is likes on a static JPEG, which selects for what photographs well in a 400px thumbnail: oversized radii, heavy drop shadows, gradient CTAs, impossible dashboards. Nothing on it was ever built, so nothing on it has an error state, a loading state, a long string, or a real dataset. An agent that learns from Dribbble produces exactly the look people mean by "vibecoded".
- **Behance** — Same disease, larger scale, plus multi-slide case-study theatre where a real screen is one image among twenty mood shots. Adobe-owned, still maintained, still the wrong input.
- **Uiverse** — A community gallery of decorative CSS components: glowing buttons, animated gradient borders, neumorphic cards, hover effects with three-colour shadows. It is a catalogue of the exact tells that mark AI-generated UI. Copying from it is the fastest way to make a product look cheap. It also hard-blocks headless browsers outright ("Sorry, you have been blocked" — Cloudflare), so an agent can't read it even if it should, which it shouldn't. Library size unverified.
- **Collect UI** — Re-indexes Dribbble shots into categories (Sign Up, Dashboard, Checkout, 404 Page, Onboarding). The taxonomy is genuinely useful and the underlying content is Dribbble, so it launders bad reference into a plausible-looking pattern library. That combination makes it worse than Dribbble, not better.
- **Layers.to** — A thin Dribbble-style shot feed. Loads, has a jobs rail, and offers nothing you cannot get better elsewhere.
- **Curated.design** — Live but adds nothing distinguishable from a dozen identical galleries. No reason to spend an agent's attention on it.
- **Lapa Ninja** — Returns 403 to scripted access; landing-page gallery heavily entangled with template sales. Skip in favour of SaaS Landing Page or Land-book.
- **Interfaces.pro** — Serves an anti-adblock detection/redirect script instead of any content. Do not send anyone here.
- **Mobile Patterns** (mobile-patterns.com) — Domain does not resolve; connection fails outright. Dead. Remove it from any list you maintain.
- **Pttrns** — Technically alive and now "completely free", but the navigation is Blog / Technologies / Troubleshoot / How To / Canva. It has been repurposed into an SEO content property with a design gallery attached. Historic value only.
- **UI Sources, Design Vault, Scrnshts, Screenlane** — All four are gone as independent products. uisources.com, designvault.io and scrnshts.club 301 to screensdesign.com; screenlane.com 301s to pageflows.com. If a list still recommends these, the list is stale.

## What surprised me

- **Every serious product-reference site shipped an MCP server, and they did it in the same cycle.** Mobbin (600k+ screens), Refero, Nicelydone, ScreensDesign, Savee (6M+ refs) and Built for Mars all expose their libraries to agents now. The correct 2026 answer to "where should an agent get design reference" is no longer "a website" — it is a $10/mo MCP connection. Savee even publishes its full tool surface and gates writes behind explicit consent. This is the single biggest change in the category and most published lists haven't noticed.
- **Four sites people still recommend do not exist any more.** uisources.com, designvault.io and scrnshts.club all 301 to ScreensDesign; screenlane.com 301s to Page Flows. The mobile-UI-reference segment quietly consolidated into two owners.
- **Typewolf has stopped.** Last Site of the Day is 14 Dec 2025 — nine months of silence, with a three-month gap before that. It is still the most-cited web typography resource in the world and it is now an archive. Fonts In Use has quietly become the live one.
- **Godly renamed itself to recent.design and started a design-agent-skills directory** with public install counts (anthropics/canvas-design at 161.4K, vercel-labs/web-design-guidelines at 29.1K). An inspiration gallery is now indirectly distributing agent tooling — and it's the most useful part of the site.
- **The best-designed thing in this entire research set is a free, unmonetized component reference.** The Component Gallery — off-white ground, real graph-paper grid, Didone display serif, hairline rules, isometric line-art glyphs, no shadows used as decoration — outclasses every VC-funded product in the category on pure craft. 60 components, 95 design systems, 2,671 examples, no signup.
- **Cosmos ships an AI-content detector with Show/Blur/Hide.** A moodboard tool treating "was this generated" as a first-class filter is a sharper read on 2026 than anything the award sites are doing.
- **The best-curated galleries are the ones with no engagement metrics.** SiteInspire shows a name and a relative date and nothing else — no likes, no view counts, no author badges. Dribbble sorts by likes. That single design decision explains most of the quality gap between them.

## Open questions

- **Mobbin's and Page Flows' actual library UI** is behind a paywall; I judged both from public marketing surfaces and taxonomy only. Settling it needs a paid account and a screenshot of the logged-in search results.
- **Whether the MCP servers return image data or only metadata** varies and matters enormously for agent usefulness. Savee documents `view_saves` for fetching actual images; Mobbin, Refero and Nicelydone do not state this publicly. A live MCP connection would settle it in one call each.
- **Cosmos pricing** — the pricing page returns "Sorry, you can't access this" to automated access. Unverified.
- **Land-book and Uiverse corpus quality** — both hard-block headless browsers, so their libraries were not inspected. A human session would settle it.
- **Httpster's current cadence** — the tag taxonomy is live and large but /2025/ and /2026/ both 404. Unverified whether it's still curating or coasting on an archive.
- **Page Flows' library size** — the widely-repeated "2,000+ user flows" figure appears on their pricing page only inside a customer testimonial, not as a first-party claim. Unverified.
- **Nicelydone's bus factor** — support routes to one named individual and the site has run since 2016. Whether there's a team behind it is unverified, and it's the main risk in depending on it.
