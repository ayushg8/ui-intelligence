# social-community

**Evaluated:** 2026-09 · **Density:** comfortable in the post, compact in the chrome — 15px post text, 13px metadata, content-sized rows, 32–36px controls · **Dark by default:** either, and you must ship both. This is one of the few archetypes where a large share of sessions happen in bed, so a theme switch is a feature, not a preference. When you ship dark, ship it *dim* — Bluesky's dark ground measures `rgb(21,29,40)` and Letterboxd's `#14181C`, never `#000`, because a feed is full of user photographs with white backgrounds and pure black turns every one of them into a flashbulb.

> A stranger's sentence has 400 milliseconds to earn a read, and the person who wrote it has to still feel safe here tomorrow.

## When this is the right archetype

The primary object is **an utterance attached to an identity**, and the primary relationship is between the user and *other people*. Users come many times a day, for two to ten minutes, mostly on a phone, and scan 40–200 units per session. The stakes of any single unit are near zero and the stakes in aggregate are not: a post cannot be unsaid, a reply notifies a human, and the product's real failure mode is not churn but harm. Two things must be legible in every unit — *who said this* and *what they said* — and everything else in the row is furniture competing with those two. Content is user-generated, so the container has to survive a three-word post, a 3,000-character one, a portrait photo, a quote-post, a link card and a moderation label, which is why almost every layout instinct imported from `enterprise-dense` is wrong here.

- **Choose this over `expressive-consumer` when** the feed of other people is the object rather than a retention accessory. Duolingo has a leaderboard and Strava has kudos, but nobody opens either to socialise. If deleting the social graph would delete the product, you are here; if it would delete a feature, `expressive-consumer` hosts and this archetype governs one pane.
- **Choose this over `consumer-marketplace` when** the unit has no terminal state. A search ends when you buy; a feed never ends. The marketplace's avatar is a trust cue on a listing card — here the avatar *is* the subject, and the item under it has no price, no comparison axis, and no reason to be in a grid.
- **Choose this over `editorial` when** the unit is a post with a reply box rather than a finished, edited piece with an author. Substack ships both on one domain: its article pages are `editorial` (Spectral serif, warm ground, 620–740px measure) and its Notes feed is this (15/21 system sans, `#363737`, 600px column, four action icons). Same company, same tokens, two archetypes, correctly separated.
- **Choose this over `technical-productivity` when** the user is a volunteer rather than someone working a queue. Slack looks like a feed and is not one: its object is *read state*, its metric is inbox-zero, and its density is set by eight hours of use. A social feed has no unread count that can reach zero and must not pretend it does.

**Mixing.** This archetype hosts more than it guests. A creator's long post is an `editorial` pane inside it (real measure, real leading, its own type scale, ending back in the feed's action row). Moderation tooling, trust-and-safety queues and admin consoles are `enterprise-dense` — a moderator reviewing 300 reports a shift needs a table, and giving them the consumer feed is the most common way community products burn out their moderators.

## When it is the wrong one

- **Team chat and workspace messaging.** Superficially identical — avatar, name, timestamp, message. Structurally opposite: chronological not ranked, no engagement counts, unreads are the object, and the composer is the primary surface rather than the feed. Apply this archetype and you ship like-counts on a colleague's standup update.
- **A support community or knowledge base** (Discourse-shaped). People arrive from Google with a specific question and land mid-thread. That needs search, in-page anchors, an accepted-answer affordance and `developer-platform` geometry; a 600px chronological column with infinite scroll fails the only task it has.
- **A comment section attached to an editorial product.** It is a guest pane, not a host. If you apply the full archetype, the article page grows a left nav, a right rail and a composer above the fold, and the piece stops being the reason anyone came.
- **An internal social intranet.** Nobody is a volunteer, so every engagement mechanic that works on a voluntary audience reads as surveillance on a captive one. Reactions on a colleague's post are performance data whether or not you intended them to be.
- **Dating and one-to-one messaging products.** No public feed, no engagement counts, and a completely different safety model (unmatch, ghost, report a private conversation). The visual vocabulary transfers; the mechanics do not.
- **Anything where the audience is under 13.** Open social, behavioural ranking and follower counts are the exact mechanics prohibited in that market. Applying this file there is legal exposure wearing a design decision.

Go to 16px only when the unit is a *title* rather than a paragraph (Lobsters ships 16/23.2 because its rows are headlines). |

If metadata sits at body size and body colour — the single most common feed failure — the eye has to parse five strings before it finds the sentence. Two steps down in size *and* one down in contrast, both. |
| Page title (profile / channel name) | **22–32px, w700**, and no more. | Control height | **32–36px desktop, 44px minimum on touch.** Bluesky: Follow pill **h33**, padding `8px 14px`; icon buttons **33×33**; action glyphs 18–20px inside a ≥32px hit area | Engagement controls are pressed constantly but are never the reason for the visit, so they get the smallest target that survives a trackpad. ## Colour

**The neutral character is cool and blue-tinted, and that is not a style preference.** Measured: Bluesky `#667B99` / `#405168`, Letterboxd `#9ab` / `#789` / `#678` / `#567`, Mastodon `#88a` / `#67678e` / `#535374`. The chrome of a feed sits beside uncontrolled user photography in every hue that exists, and a warm grey visibly tints against it while a blue-grey recedes. This is the exact opposite of `editorial`, where a warm paper ground is doing a job — and it is the reason a feed built with the corpus's default warm ramp (`system/3-tokens.md`, hue 90) reads faintly wrong even when every other number is right. Shift the neutral hue to ~250 and drop chroma to near zero.

**You need more grey steps than any other archetype — six or seven text levels, not three.** The unit has a real ladder: author name → post text → handle → timestamp → count → label → separator. Letterboxd ships exactly this as named tokens. The alternative is what happens by default: everything below the post text collapses into one `gray-500`, and the user cannot tell a timestamp from a moderation label. Borrow Lobsters' discipline for the bottom of the ladder — name the token after its contrast ratio (`--fg-4-5`, `--fg-6`, `--fg-7-5`) so nobody can invent a 3:1 grey inside a component.

**One accent, with exactly three permitted jobs:** the primary action (Post, Follow — Bluesky `#006AFF`), links and @-mentions, and the *active* state of an engagement toggle. **The accent is forbidden as the resting state of engagement affordances**, and this is the single most consequential colour rule in this file. Bluesky's like, repost and reply icons are all `#667B99` at rest and only take colour after you press them. A row of pink hearts and green arrows before anyone has touched anything is not an interface offering an action, it is an interface asking for one — and users read the difference immediately even if they cannot name it.

**Separate safety semantics from status semantics.** Moderation labels, content warnings, muted and blocked states, "this account no longer exists" — these get a quiet neutral chip, never danger-red, because red reads as *your* mistake and these are not. Reserve red for destructive actions the user is about to take (delete, block, submit a report). Letterboxd's split is worth copying: green (`#00E054` content / `#00AC1C` surface) for watched-and-positive, orange (`#FF8000`) for ratings, blue (`#40BCF4`) for links — identity and status colours are a distinct family from action colours.

**Light and dark are both first-class.** Ship three themes if you can (Bluesky ships light / dim / dark); dim measured at `rgb(21,29,40)` with surfaces at `#1C2736` and `#222E3F`. In dark, borders carry almost all the structure — Bluesky's dark build shows 96 elements at `1px rgb(72,91,117)` — and user-uploaded media needs a 1px border it does not need in light, or a white-background image bleeds into the ground.

## Type

**One family, drawn for text at 13–15px, and no display face in the feed.** Mastodon is instructive: it loads `Cal Sans UI` for headings and refuses to let it near a post. A feed that renders user sentences in a personality face makes every user sound like the platform.

**The scale's shape is the inverse of `editorial`: many close steps, very low contrast between them.** Mastodon's ramp is 11 / 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 / 16 / 20 / 22 / 26 — nine steps inside a 15px range. That is correct here and would be absurd in an article, because the page holds sixty instances of the *same* object and the differences that matter are within a post, not between sections. Hierarchy in a feed is carried by **colour and weight**, with size doing the last 20% of the work. If your h1-to-body ratio is above about 2.2, you have imported reading-surface geometry into a scanning surface.

**Weights: 400 for post text, 600 for display names, 700 reserved for numbers and uppercase labels.** Names at 600 rather than 700 matters more than it sounds — at 15px, w700 next to w400 body makes the name shout over the sentence it belongs to.

**Numerals.** Engagement counts abbreviate (`8.5K`, `63K`, measured) and should therefore *not* be tabular — tabular figures in an abbreviated count produce gaps around the `1`s and, worse, right-alignment invites the eye to compare rows, which is precisely the comparison you do not want to make easy. Timestamps switch representation by age, and the switch is a real decision: relative under ~24h (`16h`), absolute with the year above it (`Oct 17, 2024`) — both measured on Bluesky. Never ship `2 seconds ago`.

**Ship a user-controlled type size.** Bluesky's computed sizes come out fractional (`13.1px`, `11.3px`) because the whole scale multiplies against a user setting. For a surface someone reads for an hour a day, this is closer to a requirement than a nicety, and it forces the scale to be defined in relative units from the start.

**Monospace earns its place in code blocks and nowhere else.** Not in handles, not in timestamps, not in counts. A monospaced handle reads as a system identifier rather than a person.

## Layout and navigation

**Three zones on desktop: a 240–300px nav rail, a 560–620px feed column, a discardable 320–360px context rail.** The feed column is the only zone with a fixed measure, and it holds the only full-width objects on the page. Everything about its priority comes from that: no other element is allowed to be 15px near-black at 600px wide.

**The avatar column is the layout's spine.** A 42px avatar with a 12px gutter creates a persistent 54px left margin down the whole feed, and that margin is where threading, "reposted by" labels and reply connectors live. Get this column right and threading, quote-posts and grouped notifications all fall out of it for free.

**Grouping is hairlines, never cards.** A feed is a list of separated blocks. Cards are correct in exactly two places: discovery surfaces where the unit is a *container* rather than an utterance (a server, a channel, a starter pack, an Are.na channel), and the right rail. Inside the feed, a card around every post is the archetype's signature failure — see below.

**When the community has rooms instead of a graph, the shell changes and nothing else does.** Discord's four-zone layout (72px server rail / channel sidebar / message column / member list) is the right answer when membership is scoped and presence matters. The type scale, the motion budget, the colour rules and the threading logic in this file all still apply; you have simply replaced "who I follow" with "where I am".

**Infinite scroll is correct here** — items are independent, the user is discovering, and there is no position to return to — but it owes you three things: scroll-position restoration on back-navigation, a URL that encodes a permalink for any post, and a reachable footer or a persistent rail, since there is no bottom of the page.

## Components

**Belongs here:** post with an avatar gutter · inline composer that expands into a full editor · quote-post as an embedded, visually inset block (not a blockquote) · link preview card · media grid with a persistent **ALT** badge (Mastodon ships it as a visible chip, which is what makes alt-text a norm rather than a setting) · content warning with one-tap reveal · reply thread drawn with a connector rail · collapse-thread control (`show 14 replies`) · action row (reply / repost / like / share) · follow button whose hover state says `Unfollow` · profile header with cover, name, handle, counts, join date · notification list grouped by actor and object · block / mute / report · feed or channel switcher as a user-owned pinned list · "N new posts" pill · relative-then-absolute timestamps · audience selector in the composer.

**Does not belong here:** cards around posts · fixed-height rows with `line-clamp` · tables · breadcrumbs · a red dot on every nav item · engagement counts shown before there is any engagement · view counts on your own posts (they tell you nothing and train you to check) · streak counters and daily-goal rings (that is `expressive-consumer`, and importing it turns a conversation into a chore) · karma or reputation scores on every row · "who to follow" injected between replies in a thread · autoplaying video with sound · a toast for every like · skeleton shimmer over a feed you already have cached · confetti on any social action · a single-line search-style input as the composer.

For each of those, the replacement is nearly always *less*: a hairline instead of a card, a full post instead of a clamp, a "since you last looked" divider instead of an unread dot, silence instead of a toast.

## States in this archetype

**Empty has three distinct versions and they are three different design problems.** (1) *A new user with no follows* — the hardest state in the archetype and the one that decides retention. It must offer **people and topics**, not an illustration: Bluesky's starter packs are the good answer because they let one tap produce forty follows. (2) *An empty profile* — say what this person hasn't done yet in plain terms and offer the follow. (3) *An empty notification list* — this one is correct and healthy, and you must not manufacture activity to fill it. "No new notifications." Full stop. The urge to inject suggested follows into an empty inbox is where engagement mechanics start.

**Loading.** Never a full-page spinner and never a skeleton over content you already have. Render cache first, then reconcile. New items arriving while the user is reading must **not** auto-prepend — that moves the sentence under their eye — so they queue behind a "N new posts" pill at the top of the column, which is also the only honest way to show that a feed is live.

**Error.** One error matters more than all the others: **a failed post must keep the text.** The draft is the user's writing and losing it is the worst thing this archetype can do. Preserve it locally, keep the composer open, show the failure inline with a retry, and never a modal the user can dismiss into oblivion. Second: a failed *action* (like, follow) should revert visibly rather than silently, because the user has already assumed it landed.

**Moderation state — unique to this archetype.** A post can be *present but hidden*: labelled, warned, muted-author, or from a blocked domain. That is a first-class visual state, not an error and not an empty. It renders as a quiet neutral chip naming the label and its source, with a reveal control, occupying the post's own row — never as a gap, because an unexplained gap is worse than the content.

**Too much.** In every other archetype "too much" means row count; here it means **rate of arrival**. A thread with 4,000 replies, a notification list that grew 900 overnight. The answers are structural: collapse sibling replies past the first two with a count, group notifications by actor and object (`Ana and 340 others liked your post`) rather than by event, and cap thread depth in the view with a "continue this thread" jump rather than by indenting further.

## Motion budget

**100–120ms for micro-interactions, 200ms for sheets and menus, zero for anything per-item.** The frequency argument is brutal and simple: 60 posts per screen, several hundred scrolls a day, so a 300ms fade-up on each post is roughly two minutes of animation the user did not ask for, every day. Bluesky's entire measured motion system is `opacity 0.1s` and `transform 0.2s`; Lobsters and Are.na ship no transitions at all and neither feels lifeless.

**The one motion worth paying for is the engagement toggle confirmation** — a ≤1.15 scale over 120ms on like and repost. It earns its place because it is the only gesture in the interface that notifies another human, so a confirmation is feedback on a semi-irreversible act, not decoration. No particle burst, no heart explosion, no count roll-up: an animated count invites the user to watch the number, which is a design decision about what the product is for.

**Explicitly banned:** fade-up-on-scroll for feed items · confetti on a follow, a milestone or a streak · number roll-up animations on counts · pull-to-refresh spinners that run longer than the fetch · shimmer over cached content · any transition on `all` (Substack Notes ships 11 properties at 0.25s on 81 elements, and it is the measured example of what this costs).

`prefers-reduced-motion` removes the toggle animation and all sheet transitions, leaving instant state changes. Nothing in this archetype depends on motion to be understood.

## Mobile

**This archetype is mobile-first and desktop is the guest.** Most sessions are on a phone, and the desktop layout is the one that gains rails it does not strictly need. Design the 390px column first; the 1440px view is that column plus two discardable zones.

**The post text does not shrink.** 15px at 390 is 15px at 1440; what changes is that the column becomes the viewport minus a 12–16px gutter, and the avatar gutter stays at 42+12. Measured on Bluesky's mobile web: identical 15/20 post text, full-bleed column, same action row.

**Navigation becomes a bottom tab bar of 4–5 items** — Mastodon tokenises it (`--mobile-bottom-nav-height: 55px`) and Substack Notes ships four (Home / Inbox / Chat / Search). Five is the ceiling; the compose action belongs as a floating button or a tab, not buried in an overflow.

**The composer becomes a full-screen sheet, not an expanding inline box.** On a phone the keyboard already takes half the screen, so an inline composer leaves the user writing a paragraph in a 32px slot with nowhere to put alt text, an audience selector or a content warning.

**Media caps matter more here than anywhere.** Mastodon's `--max-media-height-small: 460px` exists because an uncapped portrait photo on a 390×844 screen occupies a full viewport and scrolling silently becomes swiping. Cap the height, crop to a consistent ratio with a tap-to-expand, and keep the action row visible with the media.

## Copy register

Second person, present tense, no exclamation marks, and the interface never speaks *for* the user or celebrates on their behalf. The register is a neighbour, not a host: it states what happened and what will happen next, especially where consequences involve other people.

- **"Nobody has posted here yet."** beats *"It's quiet in here… 🦗 Be the first to break the silence!"* — the generic version performs enthusiasm at someone who has just found an empty room.
- **"You blocked @ana.bsky.social. They can't see your posts or reply to you."** beats *"User blocked ✓"* — a safety action must state its actual effect, because the user is asking "am I safe now", not "did the button work".
- **"Hidden by a label from Bluesky Moderation Service. Show anyway."** beats *"Sensitive content"* — name the label and who applied it. Anonymous moderation is what makes users believe the platform is arbitrary.
- **"Delete this post? Replies to it will stay up, without your post."** beats *"Are you sure? This cannot be undone."* — the consequence people actually don't anticipate is what happens to the conversation, not what happens to their post.
- **"Only your followers can see this."** beats *"Privacy: Followers"* — audience is stated as a fact about who is watching, in the composer, before the post exists.

Counts are plain nouns: `34.8M followers`, `862 posts` (measured). Never "fans", never "family", never a count phrased as an achievement. And handles are shown in full — truncating an identity to fit a row is a trust failure disguised as a layout fix.

## The characteristic failure

**The Twitter-skin with a dopamine surcharge.** A team wires a feed endpoint to a `Card` component, adds four icons with counts, and ships. It is recognisable instantly and every symptom is a default that was never chosen:

- **Every post is a card** — `bg-white rounded-2xl border shadow-sm p-4` inside a `space-y-4` — so sixty shadowed rectangles stack down a scroll, each announcing itself twice. The references separate posts with a 1px hairline and nothing else; Bluesky ships one shadow spec on the entire page.
- **Fixed-height rows with `line-clamp-3`**, imported from a dashboard, turning every post into a truncated teaser and destroying the only thing a feed does.
- **Metadata is at body size and body colour.** Name, handle, timestamp and counts all render at 14px `gray-500`, so five strings must be parsed before the eye finds the sentence. The measured products put metadata two steps down in size *and* one down in contrast.
- **The engagement row is coloured at rest** — pink heart, green repost, blue reply, before anyone has touched anything. The interface is asking rather than offering.
- **Counts are everywhere and permanent**: followers on every row of every list, view counts on your own posts, a number on every tab, a red dot on every nav item including ones with nothing new. The badge stops meaning anything by the third screen.
- **Threading by `margin-left` with a coloured border per level**, so at depth six the text column is 180px wide and the conversation is unreadable exactly where it got interesting.
- **Motion on every item**: fade-up on scroll, a bursting heart, a rolling count. Two minutes a day of animation nobody asked for.
- **The composer is a single-line input that expands**, so there is no place for alt text, a content warning or an audience selector — which means the product has decided those are advanced features, which means nobody will use them.
- **The empty state is a ghost illustration reading "It's quiet here!"** with no path to fill it, on the exact screen that decides whether the user returns.
- **There is no moderation surface at all.** No block, no mute, no report, no reply controls, no way to see who can see a post. It is on the roadmap as "v2", which is the point at which the product becomes unsafe for the people it most needs.

**Self-diagnosis, in order of severity.** Open the feed at 1440 and check:

1. Count `box-shadow` and `border-radius ≥ 12px` occurrences on post containers. The correct answer for both is zero.
2. Measure the feed column. Above ~700px or full-width and you have no measure at all.
3. Sample the post text and the timestamp. If they are within one size step and one contrast step of each other, the hierarchy does not exist.
4. Look at the engagement icons before interacting. If any of them is coloured, you are asking.
5. Open a six-deep thread. Measure the text column at the deepest reply; under 400px means indentation is your threading model and it has failed.
6. Scroll fast and watch for anything that animates per item.
7. Try to post an image. If alt text is not reachable in one tap from the composer's first state, it is not a feature.
8. Try to block yourself, mute a word, and limit who can reply. If any of the three is missing, the archetype is not implemented.

**The root cause is always the same:** the team imitated a feed's *appearance* — avatar, name, timestamp, four icons — without deciding what the feed is *for*. The archetype's actual content is two propositions: a stranger has 400ms to earn a read (which is a typographic and density problem), and a person must still feel safe posting tomorrow (which is a component and copy problem). Cards, counts and confetti are what you get when neither proposition was ever written down.

## Signature decisions that fit here

1. **Threading by connector, not indentation.** A 2px rail centred under the avatar column carries depth; siblings past the first two collapse behind `show 14 replies`; a deep sub-thread becomes a "continue this thread" jump rather than another 24px of margin. Every reply keeps one left edge and the full 600px measure. (Measured on Bluesky: every avatar at `x=436` regardless of depth.)
2. **The feed switcher as a user-owned object.** Pinned, reorderable, named feeds across the top of the column — including at least one strictly chronological — so "what am I being shown" is a setting the user holds rather than a property of the company. This is the structural answer to ranking distrust, and it is a layout decision, not a policy one.
3. **The composer states its consequences before the post exists.** Audience selector, alt-text field and content warning are all in the composer's *first* state, not behind an overflow menu, because the post is the only irreversible artefact the product produces. Mastodon's visible **ALT** badge on published media is the enforcement half of the same decision: the norm is created by making the absence visible.
4. **A named metadata ladder in tokens.** Five or six text colours — `name`, `body`, `handle`, `time`, `count`, `label` — each with a fixed contrast target and, following Lobsters, a name that *is* the ratio. It is the only reliable way to stop a component author inventing a seventh grey at 3:1 six months in.
5. **Notifications grouped by actor and object, with a "since you last looked" divider** instead of per-item unread dots and a permanent badge. Grouping turns 900 events into 40 lines, and a single divider tells the user where to stop reading — which is the honest version of what a badge count pretends to do.
