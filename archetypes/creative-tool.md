# creative-tool

**Evaluated:** 2026-09 · **Density:** compact chrome, open canvas · **Dark by default:** either, and the decision is not a style choice — the chrome's luminance should match the medium the artifact will live in. Light for UI, print, whiteboards, documents (Figma, tldraw, Excalidraw). Dark for video, game art, 3D, photographic and HDR work (Cavalry, Rive, Photopea, Blender). Either way the chrome must be **chroma-free**; a blue-tinted dark theme biases every colour judgement the user makes in it.

> A person sits inside this app for two to eight hours looking at *their thing*, and every pixel of chrome, every millisecond of animation and every word of encouragement is a tax levied on the only thing they came for.

## When this is the right archetype

The user's artifact — a file, a board, a composition, a scene — is the interface's entire reason to exist. They are an expert or fast becoming one, they open the app most working days, they perform a small vocabulary of operations (select, move, set a value, undo) thousands of times a session, and nearly everything is reversible because the undo stack is the safety model. The product's competitive claim is *directness*: the distance between deciding a rectangle should be 8px further left and it being 8px further left. There is a viewport with zoom and pan, a selection model, and a properties surface that reflects the selection. If those three exist, you are here.

- **Choose this over `technical-productivity` when** the primary object is spatial and directly manipulated rather than a row in a queue. Both are keyboard-first and compact; the difference is that Linear's user *acts on a list* and Figma's user *acts on a coordinate space*. Diagnostic: if the answer to "where is the thing I'm editing" is a scroll position, you are in technical-productivity. If it is a pan/zoom transform, you are here — and you owe the user zoom-invariant overlays, cursor-anchored zoom and marquee selection, none of which a list product needs.
- **Choose this over `analytics-bi` when** the artifact belongs to the user, not the warehouse. A notebook or query canvas *looks* like this archetype and isn't: its user is asking a question about the product's data and will leave with an answer, not a file. Analytics-bi hosting a `creative-tool` query pane is the correct mix; making the whole BI product behave like Figma is not.
- **Choose this over `ai-product` when** direct manipulation is the primary loop and generation is one of the tools. Spline's homepage now opens with a prompt box, and it is worth being honest about what that implies: if the only path to the artifact is a prompt and the canvas is a preview of what came back, the host archetype is `ai-product` and this file governs the preview pane only. If the user prompts once and then spends forty minutes nudging control points, the host is this one and the agent is a docked panel (Framer's model) rather than an orb floating over the artwork.
- **Choose the consumer variant of this archetype when** the session is twenty minutes a month rather than six hours a day. Canva-class tools deliberately invert almost everything below — a wide rail of *labeled* icon cells (74.5 × 72px measured on Polotno, against a pro tool's 32–48px unlabeled strip), a template gallery as the first screen, a contextual toolbar over the artboard instead of a dense right panel. That is not a worse version of this archetype; it is the correct answer to a different frequency.

## When it is the wrong one

**The "canvas" that is a layout builder.** Dashboard-widget arrangers, email builders, form builders, no-code page editors. The surface drags and snaps, so it looks like it belongs here, but the user opens it four times a year. A glyph-inside-field properties panel with scrub-drag and no labels is unreadable to someone who has never seen one, and they will never see it often enough to learn. Give them labeled fields, 36–40px rows and a visible Save.

**Meeting whiteboards inside a non-canvas product.** The retro board bolted onto a project tool is used by people who are not there to draw. Ship Excalidraw's forgiving end — big tool buttons, no modifier chords, everything one click — not tldraw's density.

**Anything without an undo stack and a selection model.** The entire gesture vocabulary here is a loan against undo: no confirmations, values commit on Enter, drags apply live. If an operation cannot be undone — publishing, exporting to a shared destination, destroying a version — it does not get this archetype's silence. It gets a dialog whose body states the consequence.

**Text-first document editors, past a point.** Notion and Craft borrow the discipline (chrome that hides until the pointer arrives) and correctly stop before the rest. A prose surface has no marquee, no handles, no zoom, no coordinate space. Importing a properties panel into a writing tool produces a formatting inspector nobody opens.

**Clinical and safety-critical viewers that happen to have a viewport.** A PACS or slide viewer is a canvas, but the host archetype is `healthcare-clinical`: the measurement tools, the laterality marker and the study metadata are not chrome to be hidden, they are the thing that stops a mistake. Take this file's zoom and overlay physics; leave its disappearing chrome.

## Reference products

| Product | Why | The one thing to steal |
|---|---|---|
| **Figma** (UI3) | The reference properties panel, and the reference *shell* — chrome as floating pills over an edge-to-edge canvas | The unit/axis glyph lives **inside** the field on the left in gray with the value in black immediately after. There is no label column at all. |
| **tldraw** | The most disciplined token system in the space and the only one you can read in DevTools | `--tl-scale: calc(1 / var(--tl-zoom))` — every overlay divides by it, so a 1px selection stroke is 1px at 12% and at 3200% |
| **Photopea** | The honest version of "Photoshop-class tool on the web", and it is fully probeable | Selection in the layer list is a **solid lighter gray** (`#5d5d5d` on a `#474747` frame), not an accent fill — the accent is not spent on something that happens 500 times an hour |
| **Graphite** *(less-named)* | Open-source node/vector editor whose entire design system is readable in the browser | Ten data-type colour **pairs**, each with a pre-computed dim twin for disabled/unconnected, so a socket's colour means "this carries a number", not "this is pretty" |
| **Rive** | Editor + state machine + runtime in one window without becoming an IDE | Collapsed panels become vertical text tabs welded to the window edge — zero width when closed, and the label is still readable |
| **Cavalry** *(less-named)* | Motion design without After Effects' 1998 chrome | Dark chrome justified by the medium — the output is video, viewed dark — and the marketing site is loud purple while the app is neutral gray. Two surfaces, two archetypes. |
| **Ableton Learning Synths** *(less-named)* | Ableton's public web instrument: the parameter-row pattern at the teaching end of the density range | Every module's title, curve preview and *modulated* values are drawn in that module's hue, so "why is this number moving" is answered without opening anything |

## The numbers

| | Value | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Body | **12–13px**, weight 500 in dark chrome, 400–500 in light (Photopea 13px Open Sans; tldraw 12px/19.2 weight 500; Graphite 14px/18) | Chrome text is read in glances between canvas looks, never in paragraphs. 13px at weight 500 on a `#474747` frame stays crisp; 400 at 13px on dark goes mushy because thin strokes lose to the halation of a bright artboard next to them. |
| Dense/secondary text | **11px** for panel tab labels, status bar and coordinate readouts; hold the contrast floor at ≈6:1 (Photopea's `#d5d5d5` on `#474747` = 7.4:1) | The status bar is read peripherally while the eye is on the artwork. Below 6:1 it stops being readable at a glance, which defeats the point of putting it there instead of in a modal. |
| Page title | **There isn't one.** The document name is a 13px editable field inside a floating pill or the menu bar; the largest type in the window belongs to the canvas | A view title someone sees 400 times is a locator. Here the locator is the artwork itself. Any 24px heading in this archetype is a pixel thief. |
| Row / list-item height | Layer / history rows **26–29px** (Photopea layer row **29px**, history row **26.8px**, both measured); property-row pitch **32px** with a **24px** control (Graphite measured; Figma's own UI3 screenshot gives the same 1 : 1.34 control-to-pitch ratio) | A real document has 40–200 layers and the list is scanned, not read. At 29px with a 24px thumbnail you get ~28 rows in a 900px window with the properties panel above it; at 44px you get 18 and the panel has to scroll. |
| Control height | **24px** inside panels, **28px** for docked rail buttons (Photopea 32×28 on a 32px pitch), **36–40px** for floating toolbar tools (tldraw 40px hit area on a **36px** pitch — hit areas deliberately overlap by 4px so there is no dead gap; Excalidraw 36×36) | Panel controls are hit after the eye has already found them; toolbar tools are hit ballistically mid-gesture with a pen or trackpad, and a 4px gutter between 32px targets is a missed click every few minutes. |
| Sidebar width | Properties panel **240–290px, fixed** (Graphite 287.2px; Photopea right dock 311px = 39px collapsed icon rail + 272px panel; Figma's panel normalises to ~291px at a 24px control). Light floating style panel **148px** (tldraw). Tool rail **39–48px unlabeled**; consumer labeled rail 74.5 × 72px cells | Two paired numeric fields plus an 8px gutter plus a gutter column for the constrain-link needs ~270px. Below 240px the fields drop under 100px and long values truncate; above 290px you are spending canvas on air. **It must never change with selection.** |
| Content max-width | **None — the canvas is the content.** The number to hold is the artwork's share of the window: **78–85% horizontally** at 1440 (Graphite measured 1148.8 / 1440 = 79.8%, with a 28px title bar and a 28px status bar), plus one keystroke to 100% | This is the archetype's single hardest budget. Every panel you dock is permanent; the test is whether you would still dock it if the user were paying rent per pixel, because they are. |
| Radius (control / container) | Controls **2–4px** (Graphite inputs **2px**, Photopea **3px**, tldraw scale 2 / 4 / 6 / **9 / 11**); panels and floating islands **6–9px**; nothing above 12px anywhere | Small radii read as precision instruments. A 12px radius on a 24px numeric field turns it into a pill, and a pill does not look like something that holds an exact value. tldraw's 9 and 11 are what you get from tuning a 40px button and a 148px panel by eye rather than by scale. |
| Border weight & colour | **Prefer no border.** Separate surfaces with a luminance step (Photopea: frame `#474747`, wells `#252525` — 22 points) or an **inset** hairline (tldraw's `inset 0 0 0 1px var(--tl-color-panel-contrast)` inside its shadow), never `border: 1px solid` | A hairline drawn as a border adds layout width and lands inside the radius incorrectly; more importantly, a lit edge next to an artboard is a false edge the eye keeps checking. Pick wells (panels darker than frame, Graphite/Photopea) or cards (panels lighter than canvas, tldraw) and never mix the two in one app. |
| Elevation | **Only things that genuinely float**: floating islands, popovers, colour pickers, context menus. tldraw's panel shadow, verbatim: `0 0 3px #00000030, 0 5px 4px #00000029, 0 2px 16px #0000000f, inset 0 0 0 1px var(--tl-color-panel-contrast)`. Docked panels get **zero** shadow | A docked panel is part of the frame; giving it a shadow makes the frame look unresolved. A floating island over an infinite canvas genuinely is above the work and needs to say so, because the user must know whether a click lands on chrome or on the artwork. |
| Motion (micro / standard) | **0ms** for tool selection, property commit, selection change and section collapse. **120–150ms** for a panel entering or leaving a window edge. **200–300ms** for zoom-to-fit / zoom-to-selection, eased. Overshoot (Figma ships curves up to ~8.8%) is permitted on exactly one class of thing: a brush-size or swatch popover | A designer changes a property value on the order of 1,500 times a day. At 150ms per change that is nearly four minutes of watching easing curves. Zoom is the one exception: it is a change of *where you are*, and instant teleportation loses the spatial relationship the animation exists to preserve. |

Zoom mechanics, which are numbers even though they don't look like it: **zoom anchors at the cursor**, never the viewport centre; scroll pans and ⌘/Ctrl+scroll zooms; overlay geometry is divided by `--tl-zoom` and content is multiplied by it. Getting any of these wrong makes a canvas feel broken inside ten seconds, before a single visual decision is judged.

Layer order needs a written registry, not ad-hoc z-indexes. tldraw's is the one to copy: canvas-background 100 · grid 150 · collaborators 245 · shapes 300 · overlays 500, and inside the overlay layer, every collaborator artefact (scribble 10, brush 20, indicator 30) sits **below** all of yours (scribble 40, brush 50, selection-fg 100, handles 105) — except their cursor at 130, which sits above everything.

## Colour

**The neutral ramp must be perfectly achromatic and it must have many steps.** Photopea measured: `rgb(71,71,71)` frame, `rgb(37,37,37)` wells, `rgb(213,213,213)` text, `rgb(93,93,93)` selection — identical triples, no cast anywhere. Graphite ships sixteen steps named by hex digit (`#000 #111 #222 … #eee #fff`). This is not aesthetic preference: the chrome is a measuring instrument surrounding a colour the user is trying to judge, and a warm or cool cast in the surround shifts their perception of every fill they pick. tldraw's very slightly blue `#f9fafb` is fine for a whiteboard and would be a defect in a grading tool.

**How much colour is allowed: almost none, and the budget is spent on the canvas, not the chrome.** The accent in *this* archetype does exactly four jobs: selection geometry (bounding box, handles, snaplines, distance badges), the focus ring on an active field, the single primary action in the window (Share / Export / Publish — Figma's blue Share button is the only filled button in the entire UI3 window), and the active-tool fill in the toolbar. It may not colour section headers, icons, panel edges, or a status. Note what Photopea does with the fourth job: the selected layer row is a **lighter gray**, not blue, because a highlight that appears hundreds of times an hour next to the artwork would keep pulling the eye off it.

**Selected state is a fill, never a border and never a size change.** Excalidraw: unselected `#f6f6f9` → selected `#e3e2fe`, same 32×32 box, same 8px radius. tldraw: solid `#3182ed` on the same 40×40 button. A 2px border on the active item shifts every neighbour unless you also add a transparent border to the inactive ones, which people forget, and the row twitches as the cursor sweeps it. The one exception is a colour swatch, whose own fill is the content — ring it instead.

**Semantic colour is confined to the status bar, the export/publish dialog and the file state.** Graphite's `#d6536e` error and `#d5aa43` warning appear in the status region, not on the artwork. The exception worth building deliberately is **type colour in a node or parameter graph**: Graphite tints every socket and property row by the *type* of value it carries (number `#c9a699`, vector `#65bbe5`, colour `#ce6ea7`, raster `#e4bb72`…), and every hue ships with a dim twin for the unconnected state. Ableton's Learning Synths does the parameter-panel version — module title, curve preview and modulated values all in the module's hue (oscillator `#23b2fe`, envelope green, filter amber, LFO red). If you take this, take the pairs too; a type palette without dim twins turns a disabled graph into confetti.

**Transparency checkerboard**: 16×16px, `#ccc` on `#fff`, plus an 8×8 "mini" variant for small swatches (Graphite). Neutral in both themes; a tinted checker makes semi-transparent fills unjudgeable.

## Type

**Face:** a neutral grotesque with real tabular figures. Inter, Söhne, the system stack, or — as Graphite and Photopea both do — a humanist workhorse like Source Sans / Open Sans, which is a defensible choice here because the chrome is meant to recede rather than to have a voice. What the face must have is a **500/510 weight that holds at 12px on a dark ground** and unambiguous `1 l I 0 O`, because half the text in this app is numbers the user is about to retype.

**Scale shape: flatter than any other archetype.** 11 / 12 / 13 / 15px — a 1.36× range end to end, against `technical-productivity`'s 1.7× and an editorial scale's 6×. The reason is structural: chrome has no hierarchy to express. A section header, a property label, a layer name and a value are all peers, distinguished by weight and colour (a bold section header, a gray glyph, a black value), never by size. Any size jump inside a panel is a row of canvas you gave away.

**Weights: two, sometimes three.** 400/500 in light chrome, 500/600 in dark. Section headers get weight, not size or uppercase tracking.

**Numerals:** `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums` on every field, coordinate readout, zoom percentage and timecode — Notion forces `"lnum" 1` for the same reason. Fixed field widths with tabular figures is what stops a value going from `9` to `1000` reflowing its neighbour, which is the specific jitter that makes a hand-built properties panel feel amateur.

**Monospace earns its place in three positions and no others:** an expression or scripting field, a hex/colour value, and a code/inspect panel (Figma's `</>` tab). It does **not** earn it in numeric property fields — tabular lining figures in the UI face align just as well and read better at 12px — and it does not earn it in layer names, where it costs you characters in a 200px column.

## Layout and navigation

**There is no shell.** Answer Q1 of `../craft/navigation-and-ia.md` honestly: the user works inside one object, full-bleed, for hours, so nothing gets a permanent edge unless it is earning its rent continuously. Two shells are legitimate and mixing them is not:

**(a) Floating islands over an edge-to-edge canvas** — Figma UI3, tldraw, Excalidraw. Measured on tldraw at 1440: file pill top-left, share/sign-in top-right, style panel top-right below it, tool island bottom-centre, zoom readout bottom-left. The canvas runs corner to corner underneath all of it, so at any window size the artwork is 100% of the viewport minus overlaps you can dismiss. Correct when the artifact is spatial and unbounded and the panel set is small.

**(b) Docked regions inside a fixed frame** — Photopea, Graphite, Blender. Measured on Photopea: 31px menu bar, 39px collapsed panel rail, 272px panel column, with panels stacked as tabbed blocks (26px tab strip, active tab a lighter fill with `4px 4px 0 0` radius). Correct when three or more panels must be visible simultaneously — layers *and* properties *and* a timeline — because floating three islands over the work is worse than docking them.

**The primary object gets priority by being the only thing in the window that is not gray.** That is the whole visual strategy. Everything else is a neutral instrument panel around it, and the discipline that makes it work is refusing to make chrome interesting.

**Grouping is collapsible sections with bold noun headers, and the section's own actions live right-aligned in its header row** (add a fill, add an effect, open auto-layout). Open/closed state persists across selections — a user who collapsed Effects wants it collapsed for the next object too. The top of the panel is stable across selection types (name, then position/size) so muscle memory survives switching from a rectangle to a text layer; only the lower sections swap.

**Cards: essentially never.** A panel is one continuous surface; boxing each section inside a bordered card adds two edges and 24px of padding per section and buys nothing. Cards are correct in exactly three places, all of them outside the editing loop: the start screen, the file/project browser, and a template or asset gallery.

## Components

**Belongs here:** a numeric field whose glyph is **scrub-draggable** (press on the `X`, drag, the value changes — the highest-value twenty lines of code in the panel); fields that accept arithmetic (Figma's own screenshot shows `(100/4)+256` typed into W); `Mixed` on every field whose value differs across a multi-selection, with typing applying to all of them; multi-fill and multi-stroke as a **list** with per-row visibility, delete, reorder and a `+` in the section header; option controls that render the effect rather than its name (tldraw's entire style panel has one text label — everything else is a swatch, a fill icon, a dash icon, `S M L XL`, or four `Aa` specimens in the actual four fonts); layer rows with live thumbnails; a persistent status bar listing the active tool's live modifier chords; a floating zoom readout that is also a menu; collapsed panels as vertical text tabs on the window edge (Rive); custom SVG cursors with a drop shadow and explicit hotspots (a system cursor vanishes against a white artboard); a command palette; single-key tool shortcuts.

**Does not belong here:** a `<label>` above a bordered `<input>` (use the glyph-inside-field pattern; keep the word only where a property has no glyph — blend mode, font family, easing); an Apply or Save button (Enter commits, Escape reverts to the pre-edit value, Tab moves in reading order, ↑/↓ nudge by 1 and Shift+↑/↓ by 10); a toast after each edit; a `<select>` for a visual choice; a modal dialog during editing (Blender's no-modal-dialogs rule is the standard — put it in a region or a popover); a top navigation bar or breadcrumb; a "Dashboard" home inside the editor; spinners over the artwork; a progress bar for anything that is not genuinely long; a 300ms panel-collapse animation; a settings page that inherits panel density (loosen it — it is visited twice a year).

## States in this archetype

**Empty canvas is not an empty state.** It is the correct state, and it takes no illustration. Excalidraw's first run draws four hand-lettered annotations with curved arrows pointing at its *actual* chrome, in the app's own hand-drawn font — onboarding that is simultaneously a demo of the output. Photopea's start screen is six actions and a strip of format icons (`.PSD .AI .XD .FIG .sketch .PDF RAW ANY`) along the bottom, turning a zero-data screen into a capability claim; Graphite prints the shortcut next to each start action (`New Document ⌘N`), teaching the keyboard on the first screen. A modal carousel with three illustrations is the generic version and it teaches nothing.

**Zero selection is the document's properties, not an empty state.** Show tool defaults (tldraw shows the current tool's colour/fill/dash/size with nothing selected, and the same panel gains rows when something is selected) or document properties. Never centre "Select an element to see its properties", and above all **never collapse the panel** — a 280px canvas jump every time the user clicks empty space is the most jarring bug in this archetype and it is entirely self-inflicted.

**Loading is chrome-first.** Render the full frame — title bar, tabs, panels, status bar — immediately and fill the artwork progressively. The panels *are* the app's identity and their arrival is the perceived load. Never gate the window behind a spinner. For a heavy asset, put a placeholder in place at the right size and a determinate bar in the status bar, not over the canvas.

**Errors name the operation and the document's safety.** `Couldn't export — the file is still saved.` The user's only real question during a failure is "did I lose work". Answer it in the first clause. A failed autosave is the one condition that earns a persistent, non-modal strip; everything else belongs in the status bar.

**Too much.** Layer lists hit hundreds of rows and half of them are called "Untitled" — the thumbnail is the identity when the name isn't, which is why every serious tool renders one and why a text-only layer list is unusable past twenty items. Virtualize, never paginate. For too many *properties*, collapse sections rather than growing a 40-row scroll: the section a user needs is the one they left open.

**View-only degrades to an inspector, not to nothing.** The properties panel stays, at the same width, with read-only values; the tool palette shrinks to hand + comment; the file name gets a "View only" chip. Hiding the panel makes a restricted file feel broken.

**Local-first honesty.** If durability is not guaranteed, say so before the user has spent two hours, the way Excalidraw does on its first screen: *"Your drawings are saved in your browser's storage. Browser storage can be cleared unexpectedly."*

## Motion budget

**May animate:** a panel entering or leaving a window edge (120–150ms, opacity + translate); zoom-to-fit and zoom-to-selection (200–300ms, eased — spatial continuity is the entire point); a popover appearing (100–150ms); a swatch or brush-size popover with a small overshoot, which is the one place in this archetype where character is welcome because it is the one control that is about feel.

**Must not animate:** tool selection (the fill changes on frame 0); the selection rectangle following a click; a property value committing; a section collapsing; hover on a tool button (no scale, no lift, no shadow); a layer row entering the list; anything at all over the artwork. And never `transition: all` — in a canvas app it will eventually animate a transform on an overlay and you will spend a day finding it.

**The frequency argument is arithmetic.** Tool switches, property edits and selection changes together run to thousands per session. A 200ms transition on an action performed 1,500 times costs five minutes of a working day and, worse, lands in the gap between intent and confirmation, which is where perceived latency lives. The same 200ms on a marketing page seen once is choreography. This is why Figma's *marketing site* has hundreds of 250ms declarations and its *editor* has almost none — same company, two surfaces, two correct answers.

## Mobile

**This archetype is desktop-first and the phone build is a different, smaller product.** Say so out loud rather than shipping a cramped node editor.

What adapts, measured at 390: tools move to a full-width bottom bar in the thumb zone and lose their least-used members to an overflow (tldraw goes nine tools + expander → seven + expander; Excalidraw twelve → eight + overflow); undo/redo and the selection action row float directly above the toolbar; the zoom readout is dropped; the properties panel becomes a **sheet at a fraction of the viewport height**, never full-screen, so the artwork stays visible while you change it. Keyboard affordances are *removed*, not disabled — Excalidraw drops the subscript shortcut numerals from its tool icons entirely, because a `⌘K` chip on a phone is a lie. Minimum tap target 44px, which is why the 24px panel control cannot survive the trip.

What should not ship at all: precision vector editing, node graphs, frame-resolution timeline scrubbing, multi-panel 3D. Rive, Cavalry, Blender and Graphite are right to be desktop-only. A read-only mobile viewer with comments is a complete and respectable product; a bad mobile editor damages the desktop one's reputation.

## Copy register

A tool's voice: short nouns, no encouragement, no personality anywhere in the chrome. The personality goes in template names, the empty state and the marketing site.

- **Section headers and tooltips are bare.** Right: `Position` · `Fill` · `Effects` · `Rectangle — R`. Beats: `Element Properties`, `Selected Object Settings`, `Press R to draw a rectangle`. A tooltip is a name, an em dash and a shortcut.
- **Name a property after the result the user perceives, not the implementation.** Right: `Sloppiness`, `Edges`, `S M L XL` (Excalidraw, tldraw). Beats: `Roughness seed`, `Corner radius mode`, `12 / 16 / 24 / 32 px`. The user is choosing a look, not setting a variable.
- **Destructive copy states the consequence before the question.** Right: `Stopping the session will disconnect you from the room, but you'll be able to keep working on the scene locally. This won't affect other people.` Beats: `Are you sure you want to stop the session?` — which tells the user nothing they were actually worried about.
- **Failures name the operation and the file's safety.** Right: `Couldn't export — the file is still saved.` Beats: `Oops! Something went wrong.`
- **A field with disagreeing values says `Mixed`.** Not blank, not the first item's value, and never a silent pick — that one is data loss wearing a UI.

## The characteristic failure

The bad version is recognisable in about four seconds and every part of it is separately fixable. In rough order of severity:

1. **The canvas doesn't behave.** Space+drag doesn't pan, scroll zooms instead of panning, zoom anchors at the viewport centre instead of the cursor, dragging on empty canvas doesn't marquee, Alt+drag doesn't duplicate, Shift+drag doesn't constrain, Alt+resize doesn't resize from centre, arrows don't nudge. These are not features, they are the physics. Without them you have shipped a mockup of a canvas.
2. **Overlays are drawn in document space**, so the 1px selection stroke is 6px at 600% and the handles vanish at 12%. Fix: divide every overlay dimension by the zoom.
3. **The properties panel is a web form.** `<label>` above a bordered `<input>`, 44px rows, one property per section, an Apply button, a success toast per edit, a native `<select>` for blend mode inheriting the OS look while everything else is custom. It is triple the height and half the value-width of the real thing.
4. **`Mixed` is not implemented,** so selecting two rectangles and typing a width silently applies to one of them. This is the failure that costs the user work rather than time, and it is the one that never appears in a screenshot review.
5. **No scrub-drag and no arithmetic in fields.** The user does the maths in Spotlight and pastes it back, forever.
6. **The panel collapses or changes width on empty selection,** so the canvas jumps sideways every time the user deselects.
7. **A slider drag writes one undo entry per frame,** so ⌘Z takes forty presses to get back before the drag. One drag, one entry.
8. **Chrome doesn't shrink.** A 64px app header with a logo and a nav, a breadcrumb bar, a footer, and a 320px right panel that can't collapse — the artwork gets 55% of the window against a real tool's 78–85%, and there is no keystroke to hide the UI.
9. **Everything animates.** 300ms panel collapse, hover scale on tool buttons, a fade on selection. A tax paid a thousand times a day.
10. **The tokens are Tailwind defaults.** `rounded-lg` everywhere, a strict 8px spacing scale, `bg-gray-50` panels with `border-gray-200`, `text-gray-500` labels, Inter 14/1.5. The real ones don't look like that: tldraw's radii are 2/4/6/**9/11** and its spaces include **20** and **28**; Graphite's inputs are radius **2px**; Photopea's are **3px**. Small odd radii read as instruments; 8px-everything reads as a web app that has a canvas in it.
11. **Dark mode as a personality** — near-black with a blue or purple cast, glassmorphic panels, a glow on the selection, over an artboard the user is trying to colour-match. The chrome is a measuring instrument. It must be neutral and it must be boring.
12. **Multiplayer as decoration** — cursors without names, names that fade before they can be read, collaborator selection halos drawn above your own handles, an avatar stack whose colours don't match the cursors, and a "3 people editing" banner that pushes the canvas down 48px. The tell underneath all of it is that there is no z-index policy at all.
13. **No status of any kind.** No zoom readout, no selection dimensions, no save state, no modifier hints. The user cannot tell where they are in their own document.

**The sixty-second self-diagnostic.** Open the tool and, without reading any code: press on a field's `X` glyph and drag — does the value scrub? Type `100/4` into width — does it evaluate? Select two objects with different widths — does the field say `Mixed`, and does typing apply to both? Zoom to 3200% — is the selection stroke still one pixel? Click empty canvas — did the panel width change or the canvas jump? Drag a slider and press ⌘Z once — is the whole drag undone? Six questions. A tool that fails three of them is not this archetype no matter how much it looks like it.

## Signature decisions that fit here

Pick exactly one. It must come from the domain, and the rest of the chrome must be quiet enough for it to read.

- **Every property value is drawn in the hue of whatever is driving it** — hand-set in the neutral text colour, keyframed in the timeline hue, expression-driven in the expression hue, modulated in the modulator's hue (Ableton's Learning Synths does this per module; Graphite does the type version). In a motion tool the recurring question is not "what is this value" but "why is it changing", and today that costs a trip to the graph editor on every ambiguous row.
- **The layer thumbnail is rendered at the layer's own aspect ratio and clipped rather than fitted**, so a full-bleed background and a 12px icon are distinguishable at a glance in a list of forty rows named "Untitled" — because in a real document the name is the least reliable identifier on the row.
- **A collaborator's viewport is drawn as a 1px rectangle in their colour, but only while they are moving**, fading on idle — presence at the moment it is informative ("she's looking at the header"), and nothing at all the rest of the time, instead of a permanent decoration on someone else's work.
- **The status bar prints the live modifier chords of the active tool and swaps per tool** (Graphite: `Select Object + ⇧ Extend + ⌘ Deepest + ⌥ Deepen │ Select Area … │ ↑↓←→ Nudge + ⇧⌘ 10x`). Chords are unguessable and unsearchable; 24px of height converts tribal knowledge into ambient knowledge, and it is the one piece of chrome that earns permanent space by teaching.
- **Compare-to-original is a press-and-hold on the canvas rather than a toggle in a panel** — in a retouching tool the question is asked every thirty seconds, and a panel toggle turns a reflex into a two-step round trip.
