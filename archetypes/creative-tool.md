# creative-tool

**Evaluated:** 2026-09 · **Density:** compact chrome, open canvas · **Dark by default:** either — and the decision is not taste. The chrome's luminance should match the medium the artifact will end up in: light for UI, print, whiteboards and documents (Figma, tldraw, Excalidraw), dark for video, game art, 3D and photographic work (Rive, Cavalry, Photopea, Blender). Either way the chrome must be **chroma-free**; a blue-tinted dark theme biases every colour judgement made inside it.

> A person sits in this app for two to eight hours looking at *their thing*, and every pixel of chrome, every millisecond of animation and every word of encouragement is a tax levied on the only thing they came for.

## When this is the right archetype

The user's artifact — a file, a board, a composition, a scene — is the interface's entire reason to exist. They are expert or fast becoming so, they open the app most working days, they perform a small vocabulary of operations (select, move, set a value, undo) thousands of times a session, and nearly everything is reversible because the undo stack *is* the safety model. The product's competitive claim is directness: the distance between deciding a rectangle should be 8px further left and it being 8px further left. Three things must exist — a viewport with pan and zoom, a selection model, and a properties surface that reflects the selection. If all three are present, you are here.

- **Choose this over `technical-productivity` when** the primary object is spatial and directly manipulated rather than a row in a queue. Both are keyboard-first and compact. Linear's user *acts on a list*; Figma's user *acts on a coordinate space*. Diagnostic: if "where is the thing I'm editing" is answered by a scroll position, you are in technical-productivity; if it is answered by a pan/zoom transform, you are here — and you now owe the user cursor-anchored zoom, zoom-invariant overlays and marquee selection, none of which a list product needs.
- **Choose this over `analytics-bi` when** the artifact belongs to the user, not to the warehouse. A notebook or query canvas *looks* like this archetype and isn't: its user is asking a question about the product's data and leaves with an answer, not a file. `analytics-bi` hosting a `creative-tool` query pane is the correct mix; making a whole BI product behave like Figma is not.
- **Choose this over `ai-product` when** direct manipulation is the primary loop and generation is one of the tools. Framer's 2026 homepage headline is "the AI design agent for every step from idea to launch"; Spline opens with a prompt box. Be honest about what that implies. If the only path to the artifact is a prompt and the canvas is a preview of what came back, the host is `ai-product` and this file governs the preview pane. If the user prompts once and then spends forty minutes nudging control points, the host is this one — and the agent is a docked panel column (Framer's model), never an orb floating over the artwork.
- **Choose the consumer variant of this archetype when** the session is twenty minutes a month rather than six hours a day. Canva-class tools deliberately invert almost everything below: a wide rail of *labelled* icon cells (74.5 × 72px measured on Polotno, against a pro tool's 32–48px unlabelled strip), a template gallery as the first screen, a contextual toolbar over the artboard instead of a dense right panel. That is not a degraded version of this archetype; it is the correct answer to a different frequency.

## When it is the wrong one

**The "canvas" that is really a layout builder.** Dashboard-widget arrangers, email builders, form builders, no-code page editors. Things drag and snap, so it looks like it belongs — but the user opens it four times a year. A glyph-inside-field panel with scrub-drag and no labels is unreadable to someone who has never seen one and will never use it often enough to learn it. Give them labelled fields, 36–40px rows and a visible Save.

**Meeting whiteboards inside a non-canvas product.** The retro board bolted onto a project tool is used by people who did not come to draw. Ship Excalidraw's forgiving end — big buttons, no modifier chords, everything one click — not tldraw's density.

**Anything without an undo stack and a selection model.** The whole gesture vocabulary here is a loan against undo: no confirmations, values commit on Enter, drags apply live. Any operation that cannot be undone — publishing, exporting to a shared destination, deleting a version — does not get this archetype's silence. It gets a dialog whose body states the consequence.

**Text-first document editors, past a point.** Notion and Craft correctly borrow the discipline (chrome that hides until the pointer arrives) and correctly stop there. A prose surface has no marquee, no handles, no zoom, no coordinate space. A properties panel imported into a writing tool becomes a formatting inspector nobody opens.

**Clinical and safety-critical viewers that happen to have a viewport.** A PACS or slide viewer is a canvas, but the host is `healthcare-clinical`: the measurement tools, the laterality marker and the study metadata are not chrome to be hidden, they are what stops a mistake. Take the zoom and overlay physics from here; leave the disappearing chrome.

## The numbers

| | Value | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Body | **12–13px** — Photopea 13px Open Sans, tldraw 12px/19.2 weight **500**, Graphite 14px with **14px line-height (1.0)** | Chrome text is read in glances between looks at the artwork, never in paragraphs, so leading buys nothing: Graphite sets leading equal to font size so a row's height is its *control's* height, not its type's. On dark chrome use weight 500 — 400 at 12–13px goes mushy next to the halation of a bright artboard. |
| Dense/secondary text | **11–12px** for panel tab labels, status bar, coordinate readouts, group legends (Excalidraw's `<legend>` is 12px/15px tall); contrast floor ≈6:1 (Photopea `#d5d5d5` on `#474747` = 7.4:1) | The status bar is read peripherally while the eye is on the artwork. Below ~6:1 it stops working at a glance, which defeats the reason it is a strip instead of a modal. |
| Page title | **There isn't one.** The document name is a 13px editable field in a floating pill or the menu bar; the largest type in the window belongs to the canvas | A view title seen 400 times is a locator, and here the locator is the artwork. Any 24px heading in this archetype is a pixel thief. |
| Row / list-item height | Layer rows **29–33px** (Photopea 29px, Graphite **33px** with a **16px indent step** per depth and a 24px name field); history rows 26.8px; property-row pitch **32px** carrying a 24px control | A real document has 40–200 layers, scanned rather than read. At 29–33px with a live thumbnail you fit ~26 rows below a properties panel in a 900px window; at 44px you fit 18 and the panel starts scrolling. Indent must be small — 16px per level survives eight levels of nesting inside a 280px panel. |
| Control height | **24px** in panels (Graphite number input 24 × 80px), **28–32px** for docked rail buttons and option buttons (Excalidraw 32 × 32 on a 40px pitch; Photopea 32 × 28 on a 32px pitch), **40–48px** for floating toolbar tools (tldraw measured **48px hit area on a 44px pitch**; style-panel swatches 40px on a 36px pitch — hit areas overlap the pitch by 4px in both, so there is no dead gap) | Panel controls are hit after the eye has already found them; toolbar tools are hit ballistically mid-gesture with a pen or trackpad. A 4px gutter between adjacent 32px targets is a missed click every few minutes, which is why tldraw overlaps its hit areas instead. |
| Sidebar width | **Panel width is a function of its contents, and is fixed once chosen.** Numeric properties: **240–290px** (Graphite 287.2px, 279.2px content). Icon-only style panel: **148px** (tldraw) to **200px** (Excalidraw). Tool rail: **32–48px unlabelled** (Graphite 32px); consumer labelled rail 74.5 × 72px cells | Two paired numeric fields (80px each), a 4px gutter and a gutter column for the constrain-link need ~270px. Below 240px fields drop under 100px and long values truncate; above 290px you are spending canvas on air. A panel with no numbers in it needs none of that width — do not pad it to look symmetrical. **It must never change with selection.** |
| Content max-width | **None — the canvas is the content.** Hold the artwork's share of the window at **78–85%** horizontally at 1440 (Graphite measured 1148.8 / 1440 = **79.8%**, with a 28px title bar and a 24px status bar), plus one keystroke to 100% | The hardest budget in the archetype. Every panel you dock is permanent. The test: would you still dock it if the user paid rent per pixel? They do. |
| Radius (control / container) | Controls **2–4px** (Graphite inputs and icon buttons **2px**, Photopea 3px, Excalidraw 4px swatches / 8px option buttons); panels and floating islands **6–9px** (Graphite panel 6px, tldraw style panel 9px, Excalidraw island 8px); nothing above 12px anywhere | Small radii read as precision instruments. A 12px radius on a 24px numeric field makes a pill, and a pill does not look like something holding an exact value. tldraw's 9 and 11 are what you get tuning a 40px button and a 148px panel by eye rather than by scale. |
| Border weight & colour | **Prefer no border.** Separate surfaces with a luminance step (Photopea frame `#474747` / wells `#252525`; Graphite frame `#222` / panels `#111`, with a 4px gap between panels doing the work of a divider) or an **inset** hairline — tldraw's is `inset 0 0 0 1px #fff` living inside the box-shadow | A hairline drawn as a border adds layout width and lands wrong inside the radius; worse, a lit edge beside an artboard is a false edge the eye keeps checking. Pick wells (panels darker than frame) or cards (panels lighter than canvas) and never mix the two in one app. |
| Elevation | **Only things that genuinely float.** tldraw's style panel, measured today, verbatim: `0 0 2px rgba(0,0,0,.16), 0 2px 3px rgba(0,0,0,.24), 0 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,.1), inset 0 0 0 1px #fff`. Docked panels get **zero** shadow | A docked panel is part of the frame; shadowing it makes the frame look unresolved. A floating island over an infinite canvas really is above the work and must say so, because the user needs to know whether a click lands on chrome or on the artwork. |
| Motion (micro / standard) | **0ms** for tool selection, property commit, selection change, section collapse — tldraw's style-panel controls report `transition-duration: 0s`, measured. **120–150ms** for a panel entering or leaving a window edge. **200–300ms** for zoom-to-fit / zoom-to-selection. **80ms** is tldraw's one indulgence (comment-pin hover) | A designer changes a property value on the order of 1,500 times a day; at 150ms each that is four minutes of watching easing curves, landing exactly in the gap between intent and confirmation where perceived latency lives. Zoom is the exception: it changes *where you are*, and instant teleportation destroys the spatial continuity the animation exists to preserve. |

Zoom mechanics are numbers even though they don't look like it: **zoom anchors at the cursor**, never the viewport centre; scroll pans and ⌘/Ctrl+scroll zooms; overlay geometry divides by `--tl-zoom` while content multiplies by it. Get any of these wrong and the canvas feels broken within ten seconds, before a single visual decision is judged.

Layer order needs a written registry, not ad-hoc z-indexes. Copy tldraw's: canvas-background 100 · grid 150 · collaborators 245 · shapes 300 · overlays 500 — and inside the overlay layer, every collaborator artefact (scribble 10, brush 20, indicator 30) sits **below** all of yours (scribble 40, brush 50, selection-fg 100, handles 105), except their cursor at 130, which sits above everything.

## Colour

**The neutral ramp must be perfectly achromatic, and it needs more steps than a normal product.** Photopea, measured: `rgb(71,71,71)` frame, `rgb(37,37,37)` wells, `rgb(213,213,213)` text, `rgb(93,93,93)` selection — identical triples, no cast anywhere. Graphite ships sixteen steps named by hex digit (`#000 #111 #222 … #eee #fff`) and uses eight of them in the frame alone. This is not aesthetics: the chrome is a measuring instrument wrapped around a colour the user is trying to judge, and a warm or cool surround shifts their perception of every fill they pick. tldraw's very slightly blue `#f9fafb` is fine for a whiteboard and would be a defect in a grading tool.

**How much colour is allowed: almost none, and the budget is spent on the canvas.** The accent does exactly four jobs here — selection geometry (bounding box, handles, snaplines, distance badges; Graphite's single saturated token is `--color-overlay-blue #00a8ff`), the focus ring on an active field, the one primary action in the window (Share / Export / Publish — Figma's blue Share button is the only filled button in the entire UI3 chrome), and the active-tool fill in the toolbar. It may not colour section headers, icons, panel edges or statuses. Note what both Photopea and Graphite do with the fourth job's neighbour: the selected **layer row** is a lighter gray (`#5d5d5d`, `#444`), not blue, because a highlight that fires hundreds of times an hour beside the artwork would keep pulling the eye off it.

**Selected state is a fill, never a border and never a size change.** Excalidraw: unselected `#f6f6f9` → selected `#e3e2fe`, same 32 × 32 box, same 8px radius, same position. tldraw: solid `#3182ed` on the same 40 × 40 button. A 2px border on the active item shifts every neighbour unless you also put a transparent border on the inactive ones, which people forget, and the row twitches as the cursor sweeps it. The exception is a colour swatch, whose own fill is the content — ring it instead.

**Semantic colour is confined to the status bar, the export/publish dialog and file state.** Graphite's `#d6536e` error and `#d5aa43` warning live in the status region, never on the artwork. The one deliberate exception worth building is **type or source colour in a node or parameter graph**: Graphite tints every socket and property row by the type of value it carries (number `#c9a699`, vector `#65bbe5`, colour `#ce6ea7`, raster `#e4bb72`…), each with a dim twin for the unconnected state; Ableton's Learning Synths does the parameter-panel version, drawing each module's title, curve and modulated values in that module's hue. If you take this, take the pairs — a type palette without dim twins turns a disabled graph into confetti.

**Transparency checkerboard:** 16 × 16px, `#ccc` on `#fff`, plus an 8 × 8 "mini" for small swatches (Graphite ships both, and a `+1px` offset variant for controls sitting inside a 1px border). Neutral in both themes; a tinted checker makes semi-transparent fills unjudgeable.

## Type

**Face:** a neutral grotesque with real tabular figures — Inter, Söhne, the system stack, or a humanist workhorse (Graphite uses Source Sans, Photopea Open Sans), which is defensible here precisely because the chrome should recede rather than have a voice. Non-negotiable: a **500/510 weight that holds at 12px on a dark ground**, and unambiguous `1 l I 0 O`, because half the text in this app is numbers the user is about to retype.

**Scale shape: flatter than any other archetype.** 11 / 12 / 13 / 15px — a 1.36× range end to end, against `technical-productivity`'s ~1.7× and an editorial scale's 6×. The reason is structural: chrome has no hierarchy to express. A section header, a property label, a layer name and a value are peers, separated by weight and colour (bold header, gray glyph, black value), never by size. Every size jump inside a panel is a row of canvas given away.

**Weights: two, sometimes three.** 400/500 in light chrome, 500/600 in dark. Section headers get weight, not size and not uppercase tracking.

**Numerals:** `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums` on every field, coordinate readout, zoom percentage and timecode. Fixed field widths plus tabular figures is what stops `9` → `1000` from reflowing its neighbour — the specific jitter that makes a hand-built properties panel feel amateur.

**Monospace earns its place in exactly three positions:** an expression or scripting field, a hex/colour value, and a code/inspect panel (Figma's `</>` tab). It does **not** earn it in numeric property fields — tabular lining figures in the UI face align just as well and read better at 12px — and it does not earn it in layer names, where it costs characters in a 200px column.

## Layout and navigation

**There is no shell.** Answer Q1 of [`../craft/navigation-and-ia.md`](../craft/navigation-and-ia.md) honestly: the user works inside one object, full-bleed, for hours, so nothing gets a permanent edge unless it earns its rent continuously. Two shells are legitimate; mixing them is not.

**(a) Floating islands over an edge-to-edge canvas** — Figma UI3, tldraw, Excalidraw. Measured on tldraw and Excalidraw at 1440: islands sit **12–16px** from the window edge (Excalidraw tokenises this as `--editor-container-padding: .75rem`), file pill top-left, share/primary top-right, style panel top-right below it, tool island bottom-centre, zoom readout bottom-left in a 60 × 40 (tldraw) or 132 × 36 (Excalidraw) block. The canvas runs corner to corner underneath everything, so the artwork is 100% of the viewport minus overlaps the user can dismiss. Correct when the artifact is spatial and unbounded and the panel set is small.

**(b) Docked regions inside a fixed frame** — Photopea, Graphite, Blender, Rive. Measured on Graphite: 28px title bar, 28px tab bar, a 32px tool rail, a 287.2px panel column split into stacked tabbed panels, and a 24px status bar; panels are separated by a 4px gap rather than a divider. Correct when three or more panels must be visible at once — layers *and* properties *and* a timeline — because floating three islands over the work is worse than docking them.

**The properties panel, dissected** (Figma UI3 as the reference, Graphite as the measured twin):

- Header rows first, and they are stable: identity + primary action (avatar, present, Share), then mode tabs (`Design` / `Prototype`, or Penpot's `DESIGN / PROTOTYPE / INSPECT`) over **one panel body**, so switching mode never changes the panel's width. Figma puts the **zoom readout in this header**, not on the canvas.
- Then the layer-type row — `Frame ˅` in bold with code/component/overflow icons right-aligned.
- Then labelled sections whose header row carries that section's own actions right-aligned (add auto-layout, add a fill, add an effect). Open/closed state persists across selections; the top of the panel (name, then position/size) is identical for every selection type so muscle memory survives switching from a rectangle to a text layer. Only the lower sections swap.
- The field grid is **strict halves**: two fields per row, or one field plus a right-aligned icon group, or 3+3 grouped icon segments. Paired fields are 80px each with a 4px gutter (Graphite, measured).
- Anything that does not fit the grid — the constrain-proportions link, a lock — goes in a **narrow gutter column outside the grid**, so it never steals width from the values.
- Full-width select rows (`Clip content ˅`) use the same fill and radius as the value fields, so selects and numbers read as one family.

**The primary object gets priority by being the only thing in the window that is not gray.** That is the whole visual strategy, and the discipline that makes it work is refusing to make the chrome interesting.

**Cards: essentially never.** A panel is one continuous surface; boxing each section in a bordered card costs two edges and ~24px of padding per section and buys nothing. Cards are right in exactly three places, all outside the editing loop: the start screen, the file browser, and a template or asset gallery.

## Components

**Belongs here:** numeric fields whose glyph is **scrub-draggable** (press the `X`, drag, the value changes — the highest-value twenty lines of code in the panel); fields that accept arithmetic (`(100/4)+256`, which Figma documents in its own help screenshots); `Mixed` on every field whose value differs across a multi-selection, with typing applying to all of them; multi-fill and multi-stroke as a **list** with per-row visibility, delete, reorder and a `+` in the section header; option controls that render the effect rather than its name (tldraw's entire style panel contains one text label; everything else is a swatch, a fill icon, a dash icon, `S M L XL`, or four `Aa` specimens set in the actual four fonts); layer rows with live thumbnails; a persistent status bar printing the active tool's modifier chords; a zoom readout that is also a menu; collapsed panels as vertical text tabs on the window edge (Rive); custom SVG cursors with a drop shadow and explicit hotspots (a system cursor vanishes against a white artboard); a command palette; single-key tool shortcuts.

**Does not belong here:** a `<label>` above a bordered `<input>` (use glyph-inside-field; keep the word only where a property has no glyph — blend mode, font family, easing); an Apply or Save button (Enter commits, Escape reverts to the pre-edit value, Tab moves in reading order, ↑/↓ nudge by 1 and Shift+↑/↓ by 10); a toast after each edit; a native `<select>` for a visual choice (Photopea gets away with one in its New Project *dialog* and nowhere in the editing loop — that is the line); a modal dialog during editing (Blender's no-modal-dialogs rule is the standard — use a region or a popover); a top navigation bar or breadcrumb; a "Dashboard" home inside the editor; spinners over the artwork; a progress bar for anything not genuinely long; a 300ms panel-collapse animation; a settings page that inherits panel density (loosen it — it is visited twice a year).

## States in this archetype

**An empty canvas is not an empty state.** It is the correct state and it takes no illustration. Excalidraw's first run draws four hand-lettered annotations with curved arrows pointing at its *actual* chrome, in the app's own hand-drawn font — onboarding that is simultaneously a demo of the output. Graphite prints the shortcut beside each start action (`New Document ⌘N`), teaching the keyboard on the first screen; Photopea lists `.PSD .AI .XD .FIG .sketch .PDF RAW ANY` along the bottom, turning a zero-data screen into a capability claim. A modal carousel with three illustrations teaches nothing.

**Zero selection is the document's properties, not an empty state.** Show tool defaults — tldraw with nothing selected shows the current tool's colour/fill/dash/size in the same panel that later shows the selection's, gaining rows rather than swapping views — or document properties. Never centre "Select an element to see its properties", and above all **never collapse the panel**: a 280px canvas jump every time the user clicks empty space is the most jarring self-inflicted bug in this archetype.

**Loading is chrome-first.** Render the full frame — title bar, tabs, panels, status bar — immediately, and fill the artwork progressively. The panels are the app's identity and their arrival is the perceived load. Never gate the window behind a spinner. For a heavy asset, put a correctly sized placeholder in position and a determinate bar in the status bar, not over the canvas.

**Errors name the operation and the document's safety.** `Couldn't export — the file is still saved.` During a failure the user has exactly one question: did I lose work. Answer it in the first clause. A failed autosave is the only condition that earns a persistent non-modal strip; everything else belongs in the status bar.

**Too much.** Layer lists reach hundreds of rows and half of them say "Untitled" — Graphite's demo document shows eight consecutive `Untitled Layer` rows, each distinguishable only by its thumbnail. The thumbnail is the identity when the name isn't, which is why a text-only layer list is unusable past twenty items. Virtualize, never paginate. For too many *properties*, collapse sections rather than grow a 40-row scroll: the section the user needs is the one they left open.

**View-only degrades to an inspector, not to nothing.** The properties panel stays, at the same width, with read-only values; the tool palette shrinks to hand + comment; the file name gets a "View only" chip. Hiding the panel makes a restricted file feel broken instead of restricted.

**Local-first honesty.** If durability is not guaranteed, say so before the user has spent two hours, the way Excalidraw does on its first screen: *"Your drawings are saved in your browser's storage. Browser storage can be cleared unexpectedly."*

## Motion budget

**May animate:** a panel entering or leaving a window edge (120–150ms, opacity + translate); zoom-to-fit and zoom-to-selection (200–300ms, eased — spatial continuity is the entire point); a popover appearing (100–150ms); a swatch or brush-size popover with a small overshoot, the one control in this archetype that is genuinely about feel.

**Must not animate:** tool selection (the fill changes on frame 0); the selection rectangle following a click; a property value committing; a section collapsing; hover on a tool button (no scale, no lift, no shadow); a layer row entering the list; anything at all over the artwork. And never `transition: all` — in a canvas app it will eventually animate a transform on an overlay and cost you a day.

**The frequency argument is arithmetic.** Tool switches, property edits and selection changes run to thousands per session. A 200ms transition on something done 1,500 times costs five minutes of the working day and lands in the gap between intent and confirmation. The same 200ms on a marketing page seen once is choreography. This is why Figma's *marketing site* has hundreds of 250ms declarations while tldraw's style-panel controls measure `0s` — same problem space, two surfaces, two correct answers.

## Mobile

**This archetype is desktop-first and the phone build is a different, smaller product.** Say so out loud rather than shipping a cramped node editor.

What adapts, measured at 390: tools move to a full-width bottom bar in the thumb zone and lose their least-used members to an overflow (Excalidraw goes twelve tools → eight plus `⋮`; tldraw nine + expander → seven + expander); undo/redo float as a pair directly above the toolbar; the zoom readout is dropped entirely; the properties panel becomes a **sheet at a fraction of viewport height**, never full-screen, so the artwork stays visible while you change it. Keyboard affordances are *removed*, not disabled — Excalidraw drops the subscript shortcut numerals from its tool icons on mobile, because a `⌘K` chip on a phone is a lie. Minimum tap target 44px, which is exactly why the 24px panel control cannot survive the trip.

What should not ship at all: precision vector editing, node graphs, frame-resolution timeline scrubbing, multi-panel 3D. Rive, Cavalry, Blender and Graphite are right to be desktop-only. A read-only mobile viewer with comments is a complete and respectable product; a bad mobile editor damages the desktop one's reputation.

## Copy register

A tool's voice: short nouns, no encouragement, no personality in the chrome. Personality goes in template names, the empty state and the marketing site.

- **Section headers and tooltips are bare.** Right: `Position` · `Fill` · `Effects` · `Rectangle — R`. Beats: `Element Properties`, `Selected Object Settings`, `Press R to draw a rectangle`. A tooltip is a name, an em dash and a shortcut.
- **Name a property after the result the user perceives, not the implementation.** Right: `Sloppiness`, `Edges`, `S M L XL`. Beats: `Roughness seed`, `Corner radius mode`, `12 / 16 / 24 / 32 px`. The user is choosing a look, not setting a variable.
- **Destructive copy states the consequence before the question.** Right: `Stopping the session will disconnect you from the room, but you'll be able to keep working on the scene locally. This won't affect other people.` Beats: `Are you sure you want to stop the session?`, which tells the user nothing they were actually worried about.
- **Failures name the operation and the file's safety.** Right: `Couldn't export — the file is still saved.` Beats: `Oops! Something went wrong.`
- **A field with disagreeing values says `Mixed`.** Not blank, not the first item's value, and never a silent pick — that one is data loss wearing a UI.

## The characteristic failure

The bad version is recognisable in about four seconds, and every part of it is separately fixable. Roughly in order of severity:

1. **The canvas doesn't behave.** Space+drag doesn't pan, scroll zooms instead of panning, zoom anchors at the viewport centre, dragging empty canvas doesn't marquee, Alt+drag doesn't duplicate, Shift+drag doesn't constrain, Alt+resize doesn't resize from centre, arrows don't nudge. These are not features, they are the physics. Without them you have shipped a mockup of a canvas.
2. **Overlays are drawn in document space**, so the 1px selection stroke is 6px at 600% and the handles vanish at 12%. Fix: divide every overlay dimension by the zoom.
3. **The properties panel is a web form.** `<label>` above a bordered `<input>`, 44px rows, one property per section, an Apply button, a success toast per edit, a native `<select>` for blend mode inheriting the OS look while everything else is custom. Triple the height and half the value-width of the real thing.
4. **`Mixed` is not implemented**, so selecting two rectangles and typing a width silently applies to one. This is the failure that costs the user *work* rather than time, and the one that never shows up in a screenshot review.
5. **No scrub-drag and no arithmetic in fields.** The user does the maths in Spotlight and pastes it back, forever.
6. **The panel collapses or changes width on empty selection**, so the canvas jumps sideways every time the user deselects.
7. **A slider drag writes one undo entry per frame**, so ⌘Z takes forty presses to get back before the drag. One drag, one entry.
8. **Chrome doesn't shrink.** A 64px app header with a logo and a nav, a breadcrumb bar, a footer, and a 320px right panel that can't collapse — the artwork gets ~55% of the window against a real tool's 78–85%, and there is no keystroke to hide the UI.
9. **Everything animates.** 300ms panel collapse, hover scale on tool buttons, a fade on selection — a tax paid a thousand times a day.
10. **The tokens are Tailwind defaults.** `rounded-lg` everywhere, a strict 8px spacing scale, `bg-gray-50` panels with `border-gray-200`, `text-gray-500` labels, Inter 14/1.5. The real ones do not look like that: tldraw's radii are 2/4/6/**9/11** and its spaces include **20** and **28**; Graphite's inputs are radius **2px** and its line-height is **1.0**; Photopea's radius is **3px**. Small odd radii read as instruments; 8px-everything reads as a web app with a canvas in it.
11. **Dark mode as a personality** — near-black with a blue or purple cast, glassmorphic panels, a glow on the selection, all wrapped around an artboard the user is trying to colour-match. The chrome is a measuring instrument. It must be neutral and it must be boring.
12. **Multiplayer as decoration** — cursors without names, names that fade before they can be read, collaborator halos above your own handles, an avatar stack whose colours don't match the cursors, a "3 people editing" banner pushing the canvas down 48px. The tell underneath all of it: no z-index policy at all.
13. **No status of any kind.** No zoom readout, no selection dimensions, no save state, no modifier hints. The user cannot tell where they are in their own document.

**The sixty-second self-diagnostic.** Open the tool and, without reading any code: press on a field's `X` glyph and drag — does the value scrub? Type `100/4` into width — does it evaluate? Select two objects of different widths — does the field say `Mixed`, and does typing apply to both? Zoom to 3200% — is the selection stroke still one pixel? Click empty canvas — did the panel width change or the canvas jump? Drag a slider, then press ⌘Z once — is the whole drag undone? Six questions. A tool failing three of them is not this archetype no matter how much it looks like it.

## Signature decisions that fit here

Pick exactly one. It must come from the domain, and the rest of the chrome must be quiet enough for it to read.

- **Every property value is drawn in the hue of whatever is driving it** — hand-set in the neutral text colour, keyframed in the timeline hue, expression-driven in the expression hue, modulated in the modulator's hue (Ableton's Learning Synths does this per module; Graphite does the by-type version). In a motion tool the recurring question is not "what is this value" but "why is it changing", and today that costs a trip to the graph editor on every ambiguous row.
- **The layer thumbnail is rendered at the layer's own aspect ratio and clipped rather than fitted**, so a full-bleed background and a 12px icon stay distinguishable in a list of forty rows all called "Untitled" — which, in a real document, is what the list actually looks like.
- **A collaborator's viewport is drawn as a 1px rectangle in their colour, but only while they are moving**, fading on idle: presence at the moment it is informative ("she's looking at the header"), and nothing at all the rest of the time, instead of permanent decoration on someone else's work.
- **The status bar prints the live modifier chords of the active tool and swaps per tool** (Graphite, read off the screen: `Select Object + ⇧ Extend + ⌘ Deepest + ⌥ Deepen │ Select Area + ⇧ Extend + ⌥ Subtract │ Lasso │ Drag Selected │ G R S Grab/Rotate/Scale │ ↑↓←→ Nudge + ⇧⌘ 10x`). Chords are unguessable and unsearchable; 24px of height converts tribal knowledge into ambient knowledge, and it is the one piece of chrome that earns permanent space by teaching.
- **Compare-to-original is a press-and-hold on the canvas rather than a toggle in a panel** — in a retouching tool that question is asked every thirty seconds, and a panel toggle turns a reflex into a two-step round trip.
